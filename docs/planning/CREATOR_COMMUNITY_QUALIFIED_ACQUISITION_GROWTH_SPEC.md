## 1. Gap selected for this iteration

Moneyverse already has detailed planning for SEO, public content, share artifacts, referral safeguards, brand promise, onboarding, D1–D30 retention and permission-to-return. Creator/community collaboration exists in those plans mainly as a format or disclosure requirement.

The remaining acquisition gap is end-to-end quality: **which creator/community partnerships should bring users to Moneyverse, what should those visitors see in the first 30 seconds, and how do we judge the partnership by activation/D7/D30 rather than reach, reposts or raw signups?**

Canonical loop:

`trusted creator/community context → expectation-matched public landing → 30–90 second value proof → one authored choice → contextual signup → meaningful activation → D1 recognition → D7 continuation → optional share/community participation`

Consumer promise:

**“The thing that interested you outside Moneyverse is the same thing you can meaningfully continue inside it.”**

## 2. Partner-selection principle

Do not buy reach first and search for product fit later. Prefer partners whose audience already overlaps with at least one real Moneyverse intent:

- simulation/economy-game curiosity;
- collecting, customization or world-building;
- beginner economic/financial literacy in a clearly simulated context;
- community projects and cooperative creation;
- fictional-company/world storytelling;
- game design, strategy reflection or educational replay.

Avoid partners whose primary audience expectation is:

- real investment picks, guaranteed-return or get-rich content;
- cash gambling or gambling-tip content;
- coupon/referral farming communities;
- audiences materially driven by bot traffic, purchased engagement or giveaway-only participation;
- harassment, doxxing, impersonation or unsafe community norms.

Follower count is a diagnostic input, not the success metric.

## 3. Partnership formats ranked by user quality

### A. Creator-guided playable story
A creator explains one fictional-company, collection, profession or season question, then links to a public-safe 30–90 second sample that continues the exact topic.

Best fit: qualified acquisition and activation.

### B. Community challenge with authored output
Participants create a collection arrangement, learning replay, season reflection, fictional-company thesis or community project artifact. Recognition should emphasize creativity, learning or contribution rather than wealth or transaction volume.

Best fit: acquisition + retention + viral artifact generation.

### C. Curated community/creator spotlight
Moneyverse publishes an opt-in public-safe story about a project, archive, collection or educational outcome. The page must have standalone reader value and transparent sponsorship/material-connection disclosure where applicable.

Best fit: brand + SEO + community proof.

### D. Creator code / referral link
Use only as attribution/convenience after the user understands the product. Do not make a code itself the product promise. No meaningful WLD/WDX advantage for raw clicks, installs or signups.

### E. Giveaway-first campaign
Lowest-priority format. Giveaways can generate reach but often weaken intent quality and increase bot/multi-account/referral fraud. If used, the prize should not create financial-game advantage and campaign success must still be downstream activation/D7 quality.

## 4. First 30 seconds for creator/community traffic

The landing must preserve the source expectation without pretending the creator controls Moneyverse outcomes.

Show:
1. the exact topic or artifact that brought the visitor;
2. one sentence explaining Moneyverse as a persistent community simulation / game-only virtual economy;
3. one proof/sample action available before signup where safe;
4. a visible creator/sponsor relationship label where applicable;
5. one next action, not the full wallet/bank/stocks/casino feature grid.

Do not lead creator traffic directly to wallet balances, lending, casino, WDX profit screens or a generic home page unless that is genuinely the disclosed context and the framing remains game-only.

## 5. First 3 minutes and signup reason

A visitor should be able to understand value without registration.

Recommended flow:

`creator/community context → short sample → authored choice → result/preview → “save/continue this path” signup`

Examples:
- choose which fictional-company scenario to explore;
- pick a starter collection theme;
- complete a tiny diversification/opportunity-cost replay;
- select a profession/project direction;
- compare two season/world choices.

Signup is justified by continuity: saving the choice, creating a durable chapter, following the thread or participating in the project. OAuth success, wallet open, ad click or creator-code entry is not activation.

## 6. D1/D3/D7/D14/D30 for partner cohorts

### D1 — expectation recognition
Show the exact topic/path the user came for and prove the choice persisted.

### D3 — adjacent depth
Offer one related piece of lore, collection, profession, world or learning content. Do not convert the cohort into a generic feature blast.

### D7 — partnership quality checkpoint
Show what changed in the original thread and whether the user created meaningful progress. Creator attribution should remain analytic context, not permanent control over the user experience.

### D14 — identity/community choice
Offer opt-in curation, display, project or community participation related to the original interest.

### D30 — durable relationship
The user should retain a collection chapter, learning history, profession/project path, season record or other durable state that remains useful even after the campaign ends.

## 7. Creator/community economics

Pay for qualified value, not vanity where possible.

Evaluation should connect spend to:

`partner cost → qualified landing → authored choice → signup → activation → D7 → D30 → LTV/contribution`

Useful commercial models can include flat sponsorship, content-production fee, capped activation-quality bonus or non-cash community support. Avoid uncapped raw-signup bounties and economic rewards that turn creators into account-farming channels.

A creator/community partnership must not sell:
- better WDX outcomes;
- better lending terms;
- casino odds or limits;
- moderation priority;
- hidden search/ranking preference;
- undisclosed editorial conclusions.

## 8. Viral loop

The desired viral loop is:

`creator introduces meaningful context → user makes something of their own → user optionally shares that artifact → recipient understands it without an account → recipient samples the same class of value → activation/D7`

This is stronger than `creator code → signup bounty → more code sharing` because it creates product proof rather than only incentive propagation.

Priority artifacts:
- curated collection/showcase;
- season reflection;
- educational replay result;
- community project outcome;
- fictional-company/world interpretation;
- profile/space identity artifact.

Default public share should exclude balances, private WDX positions, debt/loan state, casino history, private social graph, account/security/recovery data and precise personal information.

## 9. SEO and public discovery

Creator/community pages must not become rented SEO inventory.

Indexable candidate:
- substantial creator/community collaboration with original editorial value;
- public-safe project retrospective;
- educational replay or world/collection guide that answers a real query independently of signup;
- evergreen archive with clear authorship and material-connection disclosure.

Default noindex/unlisted candidate:
- thin campaign landing;
- referral/code pages;
- giveaway entry pages;
- personalized campaign state;
- private or low-trust UGC;
- pages whose primary purpose is to transfer another party’s search reputation.

Organic success remains `qualified visit → value → activation → D7/D30`, not indexed-page count.

## 10. Monetization boundary

Creator traffic is not permission to monetize earlier.

Rules:
- deliver the promised content/sample before unrelated ads or subscription pressure;
- clearly identify paid/sponsored material;
- no native ad that imitates buy/sell/loan/repay controls;
- no creator-specific paid economic advantage;
- do not use private economy behavior, losses, debt or casino activity for creator-targeted advertising;
- do not pay users meaningful WLD/WDX merely for viewing, liking, reposting, following or signing up through a creator.

Measure sponsor revenue together with bounce, activation, D7/D30, ad-induced churn, support cost and trust complaints.

## 11. Experiment backlog

### Experiment A — contextual creator landing vs generic home
Hypothesis: expectation preservation increases meaningful activation and D7.
Target cohort: first-time creator/community referrals.
Control: generic home/start page.
Treatment: source-specific public-safe context + one sample + one CTA.
Primary metric: landing → meaningful activation; D7.
Guardrails: bounce, finance-like misunderstanding, fake signup, privacy complaint, phishing report.
Minimum observation: D7-matured cohort; D30 before broad scale.
Next action: scale only if retained quality improves, even if raw signup falls.

### Experiment B — authored challenge artifact vs follow/repost giveaway
Hypothesis: making an artifact produces fewer but higher-quality users.
Primary: participant → activation → D7 and artifact share → recipient activation.
Guardrails: bot/multi-account rate, spam/report, moderation load.
Minimum observation: complete campaign plus D7 maturity.

### Experiment C — micro/niche fit vs larger broad-reach partner
Hypothesis: audience-product fit can outperform raw reach on CAC-to-D30 contribution.
Primary: fraud-adjusted CAC per D30 retained activated user.
Guardrails: brand-safety incidents, disclosure complaints, refund/support burden.
Minimum observation: comparable creative and D30 maturity.

### Experiment D — explicit sponsorship framing vs weak/ambiguous framing
Primary: trust/comprehension plus activation/D7.
Guardrails: material-connection complaint, bounce, negative feedback.
Success condition: clear disclosure must not be weakened merely to improve CTR.

### Experiment E — reward-free referral attribution vs raw-signup economic bounty
Primary: activated retained user and fraud-adjusted CAC.
Guardrails: fake signup, referral fraud, suspicious reward duplication, linked-account abuse.
Minimum observation: D7/D30 cohorts.
Default: reward-free/cosmetic-prestige direction unless strong evidence supports a safe alternative.

## 12. KPI framework

Acquisition quality:
- qualified creator/community visits;
- landing → sample;
- sample → authored choice;
- contextual signup conversion;
- signup → meaningful activation;
- time-to-first-value;
- source-intent preservation after auth.

Retention quality:
- D1 exact-source-interest recognition;
- D3 adjacent-depth action;
- D7 original-thread continuation;
- D14 identity/community participation;
- D30 durable record;
- returning-user share and sessions/user for partner cohorts.

Viral/community:
- artifact creation rate;
- opt-in share rate;
- recipient engaged visit → activation → D7;
- second-generation share rate;
- report/block/moderation rate.

Economics:
- partner spend;
- CAC per meaningful activation;
- fraud-adjusted CAC;
- D7/D30 retained CAC;
- cohort LTV and contribution margin;
- creator/community-assisted revenue after retention.

Trust/security guardrails:
- fake-signup rate;
- referral fraud rate;
- suspicious reward duplication;
- ATO/phishing signal rate;
- impersonation/report rate;
- privacy complaint rate;
- sponsor-disclosure complaint rate;
- bot/fake-engagement signal rate.

## 13. Security, abuse and privacy review

### High — creator/official impersonation and phishing
User impact: visitors may trust fake creator codes, fake Moneyverse landing pages or fake reward/account messages.
Abuse scenario: attacker copies campaign branding and asks for credentials, OAuth consent, recovery information or payment.
Minimum protection: canonical-domain consistency, clear official/creator identity, no credential requests in growth messages, safe outbound-link policy, no secrets/session/recovery data in URLs.
Separate development/QA: required for actual creator deep links, campaign domains or account-linked rewards.

### High — referral and giveaway multi-account farming
User impact: degraded economy/community and misleading acquisition metrics.
Abuse scenario: bots/device farms create accounts, replay claims or coordinate invitations.
Minimum protection: no meaningful economic reward for raw click/follow/share/signup; fraud-adjusted attribution; bounded/non-P2W rewards if ever introduced.
Separate development/fraud QA: required before economic referral/giveaway rewards.

### High — finance-like creator deception
User impact: users may interpret WLD/WDX, banking, stocks or casino as real income/investment/gambling opportunity.
Abuse scenario: partner uses profit claims, guaranteed-return framing or hides game-only status to improve conversion.
Minimum protection: approved claims, nearby game-only framing, prohibited-claim list, termination/removal path for unsafe creative.
Separate legal/trust review: required for finance-adjacent campaigns.

### High — public/private leakage through creator showcases
Minimum protection: public-safe allowlist, explicit opt-in, reversible visibility, no private balances/positions/debt/social graph/security/recovery/moderation state.
Separate privacy/security QA: required before personalized public showcases.

### Medium — fake social proof / purchased engagement
Minimum protection: do not buy or reward fake followers/views; treat suspicious traffic as invalid for payout and experiment success.

### Medium — harassment/doxxing in community challenges
Minimum protection: bounded prompts, block/report/takedown, no forced real identity/contact details, moderation capacity before scale.

### Medium — analytics overcollection
Minimum protection: purpose-limited source attribution; do not transmit private economy fields, credentials, security/recovery state or unnecessary persistent identifiers to creators/ad/analytics partners.

Existing authentication, session, RBAC, ledger, admin and privacy boundaries remain unchanged.

## 14. Legal/policy cautions

- Korea: economic/material relationships in recommendations/endorsements must be clearly disclosed under the Fair Trade Commission’s endorsement/advertising guidance; do not hide sponsorship behind vague wording.
- U.S.: FTC endorsement guidance and the Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule make truthful experience, material-connection disclosure and avoidance of fake/incentivized-positive social proof important guardrails.
- Do not condition review/testimonial rewards on positive sentiment.
- WLD/WDX remain virtual/simulated/game-only; creator creative must not imply cash redemption, deposits, real securities, guaranteed income or real gambling winnings.
- Minor-facing campaigns, personalized ads, external tracking SDKs or real-value prizes require separate current Korea/U.S. review before launch.

## 16. Runtime Product Reality Audit

Verified 2026-09-14 on the public runtime:
- `/` is reachable and clearly states WLD/rewards are game-only virtual data;
- the home still prioritizes wallet, minigames, exchange, shop and quests, with several sponsored placements;
- Monthly Updates still says reviewed public updates are being prepared;
- `/announcements` is reachable but currently has no published notice while a sponsored placement is present;
- `/guide` remains broad and finance/wealth-led, including compound deposits, bonds, lending, virtual-stock gains/dividends, business income, casino and a “representative capitalist” progression narrative.

Therefore creator/community campaigns should **not** scale to generic home/guide traffic yet. The partnership promise should land on a context-preserving public-safe experience, and expectation-match must be measured before paid/community acquisition expansion.
