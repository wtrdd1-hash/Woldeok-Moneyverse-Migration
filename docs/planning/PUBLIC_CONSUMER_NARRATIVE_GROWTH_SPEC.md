## 1. Gap selected

The largest remaining growth gap is **consumer-story continuity from public discovery to the first retained relationship**.

The live home already says newcomers can look around before sign-in and then start one thing at a time. The live getting-started guide, however, still presents a much denser wealth-accumulation story: compound deposits, bonds, loans, capital gains, dividends, passive income, multiple professions, businesses, stocks, shop and casino, culminating in becoming a “representative capitalist.”

That mismatch weakens four critical questions:

1. Why should a first-time visitor care before signing up?
2. What is the one understandable thing to do in the first three minutes?
3. Why is creating an account worth it?
4. What personal continuity should make the user return tomorrow and next week?

Consumer promise for this version:

**Start one meaningful thread → make it yours → see it grow → return to continue it.**

Moneyverse remains a community virtual-economy game. WLD/WDX are virtual/simulated/game-only and must never be framed as real investment, savings, deposit, cash redemption or guaranteed return.

## 3. Canonical public story

Moneyverse should be understandable in four layers.

### Layer A — one sentence

**A community virtual-economy game where your choices become a personal history of work, learning, collections, spaces, seasons and community projects.**

### Layer B — three proof points

A first-time visitor should see no more than three equal-weight proofs:

1. **Do something:** complete a small job, learning action or collection step.
2. **Make it yours:** choose an interest, profession, collection or personal-space direction.
3. **Come back to continue:** see what changed in your thread, the world or the next season.

Do not lead with a complete feature inventory.

### Layer C — one public preview

Before sign-up, offer one safe 30–90 second preview such as:
- a fictional-company story plus one simulated interpretation question;
- one starter collection chapter;
- one profession story and example task;
- one season/world change card.

The preview must not create real account assets, expose private data, or weaken authentication boundaries.

### Layer D — contextual sign-up reason

Sign-up should preserve the value the user just saw:
- “Save this collection path.”
- “Continue this profession chapter.”
- “Remember this fictional company.”
- “Keep this learning thread.”

Avoid a generic sign-up wall when a contextual continuation reason is available.

## 4. First 30 seconds, three minutes and first session

### First 30 seconds

The visitor should understand:
- this is fictional/game-only value;
- Moneyverse combines community, progression and a virtual economy;
- they do not need to learn banking, stocks, business and casino at once;
- they can preview one meaningful activity before committing.

### First three minutes

Show one route only by default:

`preview → choose/confirm one interest → one tiny meaningful action → show what can continue`

Do not require a new user to understand compound interest, bonds, loans, market trading, businesses and casino before receiving first value.

### First session

The session should end with:
- one completed meaningful action;
- one visible proof that it was remembered;
- one next thread;
- one clear reason to return.

Primary activation is not merely “logged in” or “viewed wallet.” It is the first meaningful action plus continuity.

## 5. Narrative rules by system

### Jobs/professions
Lead with identity, mastery and contribution. WLD is a reward, not the sole reason to choose a profession.

### Collections/shop
Lead with completion, curation and expression. Avoid implying that productivity boosts are the main long-term aspiration.

### Virtual stocks
Lead with fictional-company understanding, journaling, diversification reflection and replay. Do not lead with “undervalued stocks,” guaranteed profit, passive income or return-maximization.

### Banking/loans
Describe only as game systems with clear fictional/game-only framing. Do not make deposits, interest compounding, credit or debt the default onboarding path.

### Businesses
Lead with building, operations, supply/demand choices and identity. Avoid presenting recurring dividends as a risk-free passive-income promise.

### Casino/minigames
Keep probability and game-only disclosures visible. Do not make gambling-style excitement, jackpot framing or loss recovery a primary acquisition/activation hook.

### Seasons/community
Use anticipation, archives, collection, contribution and personal history as return reasons without punitive FOMO.

## 6. Retention bridge

The public narrative must connect directly to D1/D3/D7/D14/D30.

- **D1 — continuity:** “The thing you chose is still here.”
- **D3 — preference:** “Your Moneyverse is beginning to reflect what you like.”
- **D7 — progress story:** “Here is what you built, learned or collected this week.”
- **D14 — living world:** “The world, season, club or project changed while your thread remained intact.”
- **D30 — aspiration:** “Choose what you want to deepen next.”

Never use missed rewards, wealth loss, rank decay or streak punishment as the default comeback message.

## 7. Acquisition, content and SEO

Public acquisition should center on useful pages that answer a real intent independently of sign-up:
- beginner virtual-economy/financial-literacy guides;
- fictional-company explainers;
- season/world archives;
- collection/lore guides;
- profession stories;
- public-safe community/project recaps.

Each page gets one contextual continuation CTA. Do not mass-create thin identity, profile, weekly-stat or referral pages for search inventory.

Organic funnel:

`search/social entry → useful understanding → one contextual preview → signup/comeback → activation → D7 → D30 → contribution margin`

SEO success therefore includes activation and retention by content cluster, not only impressions or clicks.

## 8. Viral and brand loop

Preferred share artifacts:
- collection chapter completion;
- space transformation;
- profession milestone;
- season chapter;
- learning reflection;
- community/city contribution.

Shareable outputs should be valuable privately before sharing. Avoid default wealth, debt, casino outcome or raw trading-return cards as the viral identity of the brand.

Recipient flow:

`understand artifact → understand Moneyverse → preview related value → contextual signup → first meaningful action`

## 9. Monetization fit

Monetization follows demonstrated value.

Preferred:
- ad-free subscription after repeated use;
- non-P2W profile/archive/space/collection expression;
- clearly labeled sponsorship around substantial public content;
- creator/community collaborations with material-connection disclosure.

Do not place deceptive subscription pressure or confusing commercial CTAs next to buy/sell/loan/repay/casino actions. Subscription terms must be clear before purchase, consent must be informed, and cancellation must be straightforward.

## 10. Security, abuse and privacy review

### High — public/private narrative leakage

**Scenario:** a public preview/share/archive accidentally exposes balances, holdings, debt, security state, recovery state or hidden social graph.

**Minimum protection:** public-safe fields only; personalized history private by default; explicit reversible public scope; private/account/security pages remain authenticated and non-indexable.

**Separate development/security QA required:** yes for any new public personalized surface.

### High — referral/share phishing

**Scenario:** attackers imitate Moneyverse share/comeback links to steal sessions or credentials.

**Minimum protection:** no secrets/session tokens/private asset data in share URLs or messages; clear official-domain branding; no “your assets will be lost” login pressure.

**Separate QA required:** yes for runtime deep-link changes.

### High — finance-like claim drift

**Scenario:** acquisition copy turns fictional deposits/stocks/businesses into implied guaranteed returns, real investment competence or passive-income promises.

**Minimum protection:** game-only disclosure near claims; no guaranteed-return wording; learning/identity framing; legal review if the product acquires real-money, cash-out or real-financial-product characteristics.

### Medium — fake activation/referral farming

Do not reward raw sign-up, page view or share with meaningful WLD/WDX. Evaluate retained, fraud-adjusted milestones and prefer cosmetic/prestige/collection rewards.

## 11. Experiments

### E1 — identity/history promise vs wealth-system promise
- **Hypothesis:** “build your Moneyverse history” improves qualified activation and D7 versus finance-feature-heavy positioning.
- **Cohort:** new anonymous visitors.
- **Entry:** public home/guide.
- **Control:** current feature/economy-heavy message.
- **Treatment:** identity/history + one-thread message.
- **Primary:** visitor → first meaningful action.
- **Guardrails:** signup rate, bounce, D1/D7, user confusion, finance-misunderstanding feedback, privacy complaints.
- **Observation:** at least one full weekly cycle and enough activated users for cohort comparison.
- **Action:** adopt only if activation quality and D7 do not degrade.

### E2 — one preview vs full feature overview
- **Hypothesis:** one relevant preview reduces time-to-first-value.
- **Primary:** preview → meaningful action; time-to-first-value.
- **Guardrails:** exploration depth, D7, error rate.

### E3 — contextual signup CTA vs generic signup CTA
- **Hypothesis:** preserving the user’s current thread improves signup → activation and D1.
- **Primary:** signup → activation.
- **Guardrails:** fake-signup rate, redirect/phishing signals, D7.

### E4 — D7 story recap vs balance/activity dashboard
- **Hypothesis:** identity/progress storytelling improves D14/D30 intent without increasing pressure.
- **Primary:** D7 recap → meaningful continuation.
- **Guardrails:** opt-out, pressure complaints, privacy complaints.

### E5 — monetization after repeat value vs early monetization
- **Hypothesis:** delaying disruptive ads/subscription asks improves D7/D30 and contribution margin.
- **Primary:** D30 contribution margin.
- **Guardrails:** ARPU, ad-induced churn, subscription complaints/cancellations.

## 14. Definition of success

This planning version succeeds when the product team can evaluate every public/new-user message with one test:

**Does this help the user understand one meaningful reason to start, one reason to create an account, and one personal thread worth returning to — without requiring them to believe that wealth accumulation, passive income or gambling excitement is the purpose of Moneyverse?**

Runtime copy, routing and UI changes remain separate implementation work and require normal product/security/QA gates.
