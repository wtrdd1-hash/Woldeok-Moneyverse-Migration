## 1. Gap selected

The largest remaining growth gap is not simply “add referral rewards.” The product already has reasons to return and several shareable artifact ideas, but the loop between retained-user value and high-quality new-user acquisition is not yet explicit enough.

Moneyverse needs a loop where:

`meaningful progress → personal recap/artifact → optional sharing → useful public landing → recipient understands value before signup → recipient activates → recipient forms their own progress story`

The loop must work without exposing private financial-game data, without paying for raw registrations, without turning sharing into spam, and without making competitive profit or wealth the main social status signal.

## 2. Growth promise

The retention-to-viral promise is:

**“What you accomplish should become a story worth keeping first and worth sharing second.”**

Sharing is a by-product of meaningful retention, not a requirement for progression.

The artifact must have value to the owner even if they never share it. This prevents the product from degrading into referral mechanics disguised as content.

## 3. Core loop

### Step A — create something worth remembering
Eligible source moments include:
- a weekly or monthly self-progress recap;
- completion of a collection chapter;
- a profession specialization milestone;
- a room, office or clubhouse showcase;
- a fictional-company thesis or learning replay;
- a season chapter/history milestone;
- a club/city project contribution or completion;
- a museum/archive/prestige milestone.

Avoid generating share prompts for trivial clicks, raw login streaks, large WLD balance, loan use, casino outcomes, or pure virtual-stock profit.

### Step B — produce an owner-first artifact
The default artifact answers:
1. what changed;
2. what the user chose or learned;
3. what they completed;
4. what they want to do next.

Examples:
- “My first week: collector path 3/8, logistics profession level 4, one market lesson completed.”
- “I finished the Harbor History collection and restored two archive items.”
- “My virtual market replay: I reduced concentration after reviewing a loss.”

Owner-first means the artifact appears in the user's own history/archive before any share CTA.

### Step C — ask for optional sharing at the right moment
Preferred share prompts:
- after the user has already seen and understood the artifact;
- after a genuine completion or reflection moment;
- no repeated nagging after dismissal;
- no countdown pressure;
- no reward conditioned on posting publicly.

The copy should emphasize “share your story/showcase” rather than “invite friends to earn.”

### Step D — recipient landing must stand on its own
A non-member opening a shared page should understand within about 30 seconds:
- what the artifact represents;
- what Moneyverse is;
- that WLD/WDX are virtual/simulated/game-only;
- what they can explore without signing up;
- one clear next action.

The landing should not immediately replace the content with an authentication wall.

Preferred recipient CTA examples:
- “Explore this collection story.”
- “Try a sample market replay.”
- “See how professions grow.”
- “Preview this season chapter.”

Only after the recipient receives value should the page ask them to save their own progress or begin a personal path.

### Step E — close the loop through activation quality
A share is successful only if it creates qualified product understanding and downstream retention.

Primary quality path:
`share → landing engaged visit → pre-signup value action → signup → first value → D1 → D7`

Raw share count, link clicks or registrations are diagnostic metrics, not final success metrics.

## 4. Artifact portfolio by lifecycle stage

### D1–D3
Goal: confidence, not boasting.
Artifacts:
- “first path chosen” card;
- first collection start;
- first learning insight;
- first customized profile/space snapshot.

Do not push sharing aggressively at this stage. Save-to-profile/history is the main action.

### D7
Goal: visible self-progress.
Artifacts:
- weekly progress story;
- one “I learned / I changed” insight;
- first completed small collection or project;
- one optional public-safe showcase.

This is the first strong viral opportunity because the artifact has context and history.

### D14
Goal: show relationship to a living world.
Artifacts:
- season/world change reaction;
- fictional-company thesis with educational context;
- club/city project milestone;
- collection chapter progress.

### D30+
Goal: identity and legacy.
Artifacts:
- monthly archive;
- profession identity;
- completed collection gallery;
- space/headquarters showcase;
- season archive/history;
- prestige/community contribution story.

Long-term sharing should increasingly express identity, mastery, taste and legacy rather than wealth.

## 5. Public share page experience

### Information priority
Above the fold:
1. artifact title and visual;
2. short plain-language explanation;
3. creator-selected public identity only;
4. “virtual/game-only” context where market/economy values appear;
5. one exploration CTA.

Below:
- related public guide/lore;
- “how this works” explanation;
- optional adjacent artifacts if public-safe;
- signup CTA tied to the visitor's current interest.

### What must never be public by default
- private WLD balance;
- full portfolio/positions;
- loan/credit status;
- email or account identifiers;
- security/recovery state;
- precise private social graph;
- moderation/report state;
- billing/subscription details;
- hidden inventory provenance;
- internal fraud/risk labels.

### User control
Before first public share, users must clearly understand what becomes public. Private-by-default should be the baseline for personalized financial-game and social data.

Users should be able to stop future sharing and remove/disable a public artifact where applicable without losing the underlying private achievement.

## 6. Viral loop without spam

Do not optimize for “invite sends.” Optimize for useful recipient outcomes.

Avoid:
- contact-book scraping or broad address-book upload as a growth prerequisite;
- repeated DM prompts;
- automatic posting;
- economic reward for every raw invite;
- guilt copy such as “your friend is waiting” when that is not genuinely true;
- misleading “official reward” urgency.

Preferred mechanisms:
- user-initiated link or image sharing;
- copy link / native share sheet;
- public-safe showcase pages;
- QR for in-person/community events where appropriate;
- creator/community campaigns with clear sponsorship disclosure.

## 7. Referral policy

Referral is secondary to artifact sharing.

### Reward principles
If referral rewards are used:
- reward genuine activation/retention milestones, not raw account creation;
- favor cosmetic, collection, prestige or convenience value;
- avoid meaningful WLD/WDX advantage;
- do not condition rewards on positive reviews or public praise;
- do not let inviters see private progress of invitees.

Suggested milestone ladder:
- recipient completes first value: acknowledgment/badge progress;
- recipient returns on a later day: cosmetic/collection progress;
- recipient reaches a meaningful retained milestone: prestige recognition.

The exact thresholds remain a product/abuse experiment, not a hardcoded promise in this planning document.

### Fraud-aware growth decision
A referral program is successful only if:
- activated-recipient quality is comparable to or better than baseline acquisition;
- fake-signup and linked-account abuse remain acceptable;
- support/privacy complaints do not materially increase;
- reward cost and abuse loss are included in CAC/LTV.

## 8. Creator and community loop

Creators and communities can convert long-term player stories into discovery without making Moneyverse a profit-promise channel.

Priority collaboration formats:
- fictional-company analysis challenge focused on reasoning quality;
- collection/space showcase themes;
- season lore explainers;
- beginner finance-learning sessions;
- community city/club project storytelling;
- retrospective “what I learned” formats.

Avoid:
- affiliate messaging implying real investment return;
- creator compensation tied to positive claims;
- paid promotion disguised as organic community opinion;
- leaderboard formats based only on highest virtual-stock profit or gambling outcomes.

Sponsored/compensated creator content must clearly disclose the material relationship in the content itself.

## 9. SEO from share artifacts

Not every shared artifact should become an indexable SEO page.

### Index candidates
- editorially meaningful public collection galleries;
- high-quality public profile/showcase pages when the user explicitly chooses public visibility;
- public season archives;
- public club/city project retrospectives;
- original educational market replay/explainer pages;
- creator pages with substantial first-hand public content.

### Usually noindex/private
- one-off low-context share cards;
- thin automatically generated recap URLs;
- private or semi-private friend shares;
- personal balances/portfolio details;
- temporary reward/referral pages;
- security/account/billing/recovery content.

Structured data should describe visible public content accurately; markup must not exaggerate private or nonexistent community information.

Organic success path:
`indexed useful page → engaged organic visit → relevant exploration → signup → activation → D7/D30 → margin`

Do not count index volume itself as success.

## 10. Brand effect

The intended brand association is:
- “I build a history here.”
- “I can show what I learned or made.”
- “The economy is fictional, but my choices and progress feel personal.”
- “I can take a break without losing my identity.”

This is stronger and safer than positioning Moneyverse as a place to “get rich,” “beat the market,” or “never miss rewards.”

## 11. Monetization connection

Monetization should enhance expression after the artifact loop proves value.

Good fits:
- ad removal;
- optional profile/space/showcase cosmetics;
- archive presentation styles;
- non-P2W frames/themes for collection or season history;
- clearly labeled sponsored public content that does not alter market/economy outcomes.

Bad fits:
- pay to boost share reach as if it were organic community endorsement;
- pay to improve WDX performance, loan terms, ranking or reward eligibility;
- disruptive ads on sensitive buy/sell/loan/repayment actions;
- putting a paywall between a recipient and the shared artifact before they understand it.

Evaluate revenue using D7/D30 retention, share quality, subscription conversion, ad-induced churn and cohort contribution margin—not ad impressions alone.

## 12. Trust, abuse and privacy review

### High — accidental private financial/social data publication
User impact: stalking, phishing, embarrassment, targeted account attacks.
Abuse scenario: a share template exposes balances, portfolio positions, social graph or account identifiers.
Minimum protection: public-safe field allowlist; private-by-default personalized data; explicit preview before first public share; search indexing restricted to intentionally public surfaces.
Separate development/QA required: yes, before launch of any new public artifact surface.

### High — share/referral phishing impersonation
User impact: account takeover or malicious-link trust.
Abuse scenario: attackers imitate Moneyverse reward/share pages or comeback messages.
Minimum protection: recognizable canonical branding/destination rules, no secrets in shared URLs or message text, no threats of asset/account loss, and user education around official destinations.
Separate development/QA required: yes for any actual deep-link/share implementation.

### High — referral farming / multi-account abuse
User impact: distorted economy, unfair recognition, increased acquisition cost.
Abuse scenario: one operator creates or controls many accounts to trigger rewards.
Minimum protection: no meaningful reward for raw signup; retained activation milestone; non-P2W rewards; fraud-adjusted CAC and manual review/escalation readiness.
Separate development/QA required: yes before economic referral rewards.

### Medium — UGC harassment, impersonation and doxxing
Minimum protection: public visibility controls, block/report paths, moderation readiness, no forced display of real identity.

### Medium — creator/sponsor deception
Minimum protection: clear sponsorship/material-connection disclosure close to the endorsement; no incentive conditioned on positive sentiment.

### Medium — analytics/ad overcollection
Minimum protection: do not include tokens, credentials, private financial-game data or unnecessary sensitive identifiers in share/referral analytics; personalized advertising requires age/region/privacy review.

Existing auth/session/RBAC/ledger/security plans remain intact; this specification does not relax those boundaries.

## 13. KPI framework

### Owner-side retention
- weekly/monthly artifact eligibility rate;
- artifact view/save rate;
- artifact → meaningful next action;
- D7→D14 and D14→D30 retention among eligible cohorts;
- archive revisit rate.

### Sharing quality
- optional share rate;
- share recipient engaged-visit rate;
- share → pre-signup value action;
- share → signup → activation;
- share → activation → D7;
- recipient D30 vs other acquisition cohorts.

### Viral efficiency
- retained users generating at least one qualified recipient visit;
- activated recipients per 100 retained sharers;
- effective viral contribution to new activated users;
- referral CAC after reward, fraud and support costs.

### Brand/content
- direct/brand search returning traffic;
- public artifact/content return visits;
- organic visit → activation → D7/D30;
- creator/community cohort D7/D30;
- user satisfaction / “worth sharing” signals.

### Revenue quality
- D30 LTV by viral/organic cohort;
- subscription conversion after repeated value;
- ad-induced churn;
- contribution margin after creator/referral costs.

### Trust guardrails
- fake-signup rate;
- referral-fraud rate;
- spam/report rate;
- privacy complaint rate;
- public-share takedown/hide rate;
- account-takeover signal rate;
- suspicious reward duplication;
- creator/sponsor disclosure complaints.

## 14. Experiment backlog

### Experiment A — owner-first weekly artifact
Hypothesis: presenting the recap first as a private personal archive increases D14 retention and produces higher-quality voluntary shares than presenting a share CTA immediately.
Target: D7 users with sufficient meaningful activity.
Entry: weekly recap.
Control: recap with immediate prominent share CTA.
Treatment: private archive/save as primary action; share offered after recap review.
Primary: D14 retention; voluntary qualified share rate.
Guardrails: share complaints, privacy complaints, recap abandonment.
Minimum observation: two weekly cycles; extend until recipient D7 is directionally readable.
If successful: make owner-first artifact the default. If not: improve artifact usefulness before increasing share pressure.

### Experiment B — story landing vs signup wall
Hypothesis: letting recipients understand the shared artifact and try one related public sample before signup improves activated-recipient quality.
Target: non-member share recipients.
Entry: public share link.
Control: artifact summary + immediate signup CTA.
Treatment: full public-safe story + one sample/exploration CTA + contextual signup later.
Primary: share visit → activation → D7.
Guardrails: bounce rate, page abuse, privacy exposure, bot/fake-signup rate.
Minimum observation: enough recipients to observe signup and one D7 window.
If successful: use value-first landing across artifact types.

### Experiment C — self-progress story vs wealth/rank card
Hypothesis: identity/progress artifacts create safer and more durable social acquisition than raw wealth/rank artifacts.
Target: D7+ eligible users.
Entry: share artifact chooser.
Control: rank/wealth-forward card where safe/available.
Treatment: progress/learning/collection/identity story.
Primary: recipient activation and recipient D7.
Guardrails: user pressure, market-manipulation signals, harassment, privacy complaints.
Minimum observation: two weekly cycles plus recipient retention window.
If successful: de-emphasize wealth/rank as default social status.

### Experiment D — referral reward at activation vs retained milestone
Hypothesis: delaying non-P2W referral recognition until genuine retained activation reduces fraud without materially reducing high-quality referral growth.
Target: eligible inviters/recipients.
Entry: referral program.
Control: reward at first activation.
Treatment: acknowledgment at activation, meaningful cosmetic/prestige progress after later retained milestone.
Primary: retained referred users per reward cost.
Guardrails: fake signup, linked-account/fraud signals, support complaints, recipient D7/D30.
Minimum observation: at least one D30 cohort before economic conclusion.
If successful: adopt retained milestone as the main reward gate.

### Experiment E — public archive SEO vs thin recap indexing
Hypothesis: indexing only substantial public archives/showcases generates fewer but higher-quality organic cohorts than indexing every generated artifact.
Target: public content surfaces.
Entry: search discovery.
Control: broader index eligibility for generated artifact pages.
Treatment: noindex thin recaps; index substantial opt-in public archives/guides only.
Primary: organic visit → activation → D7/D30.
Guardrails: indexing/privacy incidents, crawl waste, duplicate-content rate, low-engagement sessions.
Minimum observation: multiple crawl/index cycles plus D30 cohort window.
If successful: keep quality threshold high rather than maximizing indexed URL count.

## 16. Product/legal boundaries

- WLD/WDX remain virtual/simulated/game-only.
- Shared market/portfolio stories must not imply real investment performance, guaranteed return or cash-out value.
- Creator sponsorship, affiliate-like relationships and incentivized endorsements require clear disclosure; Korea/U.S. market-specific review is required before launch.
- Personalized advertising, minors, cross-border tracking, contact uploads, new analytics/ad vendors and public UGC expansion require privacy/trust review before adoption.
- Public artifact/profile indexing is opt-in/intentional and must not expose private account, financial-game, security, recovery, moderation or billing state.
- Runtime code, DB, API, authentication and infrastructure changes are outside this growth-planning update.

## 18. Next growth priority

After this loop is defined, the next largest consumer-growth question is likely **brand/content cadence**: what recurring public editorial and seasonal content makes a non-player or lapsed player return to Moneyverse even when they are not currently playing, and how that content converts into high-quality activation without becoming thin SEO inventory.
