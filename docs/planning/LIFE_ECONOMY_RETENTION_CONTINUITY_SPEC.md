## 0. Objective

The retention system must make users want to return because their economic life is progressing, relationships matter, choices have visible consequences, the world changes, and future goals feel attainable.

Retention is not defined as maximizing raw session count. The product optimizes for:
- voluntary return;
- meaningful progress;
- broad feature adoption without forced usage;
- durable social ties;
- clear next goals;
- safe recovery after absence;
- long-term world continuity;
- healthy notification opt-in;
- low regret and low compulsion.

## 1. Motivation architecture

Moneyverse uses three durable motivation pillars:

### 1.1 Autonomy
Users choose among viable paths:
- employee;
- specialist;
- entrepreneur;
- investor/saver;
- public/community contributor;
- city mover;
- mentor;
- partnership leader.

No path should be objectively mandatory for progression.

### 1.2 Competence
Every important system provides:
- visible progress;
- skill mastery;
- increasingly difficult but achievable challenges;
- feedback explaining why outcomes occurred;
- recovery guidance after failure.

### 1.3 Relatedness
Long-term return is strengthened through:
- co-business;
- teams/partnerships;
- city communities;
- public projects;
- mentorship;
- friendly rivals;
- season groups;
- shared milestones.

Social mechanics must create cooperation and identity, not only comparison.

## 2. Multi-horizon goal stack

Every active user should normally have one goal visible at each horizon:

### Now: 30 seconds–5 minutes
Examples:
- review today's cash-flow;
- make one economic choice;
- resolve one business alert;
- accept/decline one opportunity.

### Today
Examples:
- complete work shift;
- protect cash buffer;
- sell inventory;
- finish a skill lesson;
- vote in a city project.

### This week
Examples:
- reach savings buffer;
- improve credit factor;
- complete 3 profession tasks;
- close a profitable business week;
- help a partner company.

### This season: 6–8 weeks
Examples:
- survive inflation shock;
- reach career tier;
- expand business to another city;
- complete a public project;
- achieve resilience rank.

### Long term: 90–365+ days
Examples:
- career specialization;
- debt-free milestone;
- diversified income;
- multi-city business;
- mentorship legacy;
- rare non-P2W prestige collection;
- historical economic record.

The UI should always show the nearest actionable step and allow expansion into the longer roadmap.

## 3. First-session retention design

### First 60 seconds
User sees:
- current starter role;
- first salary opportunity;
- one expense;
- one choice;
- one visible future goal.

Avoid long lore before agency.

### First 5 minutes
Complete one full economic cycle:
`earn -> obligation -> choice -> result -> progress`.

### First 15 minutes
Unlock:
- first Career Book entry;
- first skill;
- first small savings goal;
- preview of a future job/company/city opportunity.

### End-of-first-session "return promise"
Show a real, non-punitive reason to come back:
- next simulated payday;
- pending job response;
- business order completion;
- city project vote result;
- personal goal update.

Nothing is confiscated if the user does not return on time.

## 4. D1 retention contract

On the next visit:
- recap what changed while away;
- show no more than three priority items;
- provide one fast win;
- show one new opportunity;
- explain progress since last session.

D1 should not depend on streak fear. If a user misses D1, D2 offers the same recovery-oriented re-entry.

## 5. D3–D7 retention contract

Within the first week users should experience:
- at least two distinct economic systems;
- one meaningful choice with delayed consequence;
- one social/community exposure;
- one progression milestone;
- one personalized goal;
- one world event or economic-news reaction.

Feature recommendations use observed behavior but never hide core options.

## 6. D7–D30 retention contract

By day 30, a healthy user journey should form a personal identity:
- chosen profession or skill direction;
- recognizable financial strategy;
- city affiliation;
- business/social connection;
- visible Career Book history;
- medium-term goal.

Late early-game should begin replacing tutorial rewards with intrinsic progression and social/world continuity.

## 7. D30–D90 retention contract

Long-term systems:
- specialization branches;
- business depth;
- season arcs;
- city/public projects;
- co-business;
- fair leagues;
- changing macroeconomy;
- prestige history.

The product must continuously open new combinations, not simply inflate reward numbers.

## 8. D90–D365+ legacy contract

Veteran users need:
- legacy titles;
- mentoring;
- founding-company history;
- city contribution history;
- rare historical badges;
- veteran governance/consultative roles;
- deep analytics;
- advanced economic scenarios;
- optional collection/prestige sinks.

Veteran advantages must be prestige/agency/information depth, not unbeatable economic power.

## 9. Daily return loop

Home screen order:
1. "What changed while you were away?"
2. "What needs attention?"
3. "What can you improve today?"
4. "What opportunity is new?"
5. "What are your longer goals?"

Maximum priority cards: 3. Additional items are collapsed.

Recommended daily content:
- economic briefing;
- salary/business result;
- one cash-flow item;
- one personal goal;
- one social/public update;
- optional Daily Choice.

## 10. Weekly return loop

Weekly Statement includes:
- income vs expenses;
- free cash flow;
- net worth change;
- resilience change;
- debt progress;
- skill/career progress;
- business performance;
- city/world effects;
- one recommendation.

Weekly Review should generate a next-week plan with 1–3 user-editable goals.

## 11. Monthly/seasonal return loop

Monthly or season statement:
- milestone history;
- best decision;
- biggest risk;
- economic environment;
- comparative percentile ranges;
- next opportunity frontier.

A season ending creates closure plus continuity:
`season result -> permanent history -> new theme -> retained core assets -> new goals`.

## 12. Quest architecture

Quests are economic goals, not chores.

Quest categories:
- learn;
- stabilize;
- grow;
- recover;
- explore;
- collaborate;
- contribute;
- specialize;
- experiment.

Rules:
- avoid repetitive click-count quests;
- no required ad viewing;
- no punitive "login every day or lose everything";
- provide multiple completion paths where possible;
- reward progression/status/cosmetics more than raw WLD.

## 13. Dynamic personal goals

Personal goals can be suggested by a deterministic planner or AI recommendation layer.

Inputs:
- life stage;
- recent cash-flow;
- debt status;
- skills;
- business state;
- city;
- user-selected priorities;
- past completion rate.

Goal suggestions must be explainable and dismissible.

Examples:
- "Build 7 days of living-cost buffer."
- "Improve delivery skill to qualify for Tier 2 logistics job."
- "Reduce debt-service ratio by 5 points."
- "Complete one joint project with a friend."

## 14. Opportunity feed

A bounded feed surfaces:
- job opening;
- training;
- supplier discount;
- city incentive;
- business contract;
- public project;
- partnership invitation;
- season challenge.

Ranking factors:
- eligibility;
- relevance;
- diversity;
- novelty;
- user preference;
- safety.

Do not optimize solely for click-through rate.

## 15. Economic narrative continuity

The world creates stories from real internal data:
- "Your city entered a hiring boom."
- "Your industry is slowing."
- "Your company's margin improved after your pricing decision."
- "The public transit project you voted for completed."

Narrative must connect past user action to present state and future possibility.

## 16. Pending-state continuity

Safe pending states create anticipation:
- job application review;
- training completion;
- production batch;
- business contract response;
- city vote result;
- season milestone evaluation.

Rules:
- timers must be real;
- missed return does not destroy earned value;
- no false urgency;
- users can see exact expected completion window.

## 17. Absence-safe progression

If users leave:
- essential debt or recurring costs must not create irreversible ruin solely from real-world absence;
- use simulation pause, grace period, capped accrual or protected minimums;
- business may slow rather than catastrophically collapse;
- missed seasonal tasks have catch-up routes;
- social partners can continue under shared rules without stealing inactive member assets.

This is critical for long-term trust.

## 18. Return-after-absence flow

For 3, 7, 30, 90+ day absence:
1. compact recap;
2. protect user from overwhelming backlog;
3. forgive/cap absence-only penalties;
4. show one recovery task;
5. highlight what is new;
6. restore previous goals;
7. offer "resume old path" or "start a new direction."

No shame messages such as "You abandoned your company."

## 19. Dormant-user win-back

Win-back triggers may use:
- completed season transition;
- important partnership activity;
- newly available job/business opportunity;
- goal becoming attainable;
- meaningful city/world change.

Do not send generic "we miss you" spam.

Frequency caps and user preference controls are mandatory.

## 20. Notification policy

Notification classes:
- transactional/required;
- user-requested reminders;
- social;
- economic opportunities;
- season/world updates.

Defaults:
- transactional only at account creation;
- ask for optional notification permission after the user experiences the relevant value;
- allow per-category opt-in/out;
- quiet hours;
- digest mode;
- maximum frequency limits.

Suggested cap:
- opportunity/social non-transactional: default <= 3/week;
- daily digest: max 1/day only if opted in;
- urgent level reserved for genuine account/security issues, not engagement.

## 21. Streak replacement

Avoid fragile streaks that punish one missed day.

Use:
- "active days this month";
- rolling 7/30-day consistency;
- grace tokens earned through normal use;
- personal-best streak history without loss of permanent progress;
- weekly consistency goals.

Missing one day should not erase months of work.

## 22. Comeback bonus design

Return incentives may include:
- temporary training acceleration;
- free career consultation;
- waived small simulation fee;
- catch-up mission;
- information/recommendation boost.

Do not create WLD inflation or punish continuous users. Comeback benefits should help re-entry, not dominate optimal strategy.

## 23. Social retention graph

Track meaningful social edges:
- co-business partner;
- mentor/mentee;
- city teammate;
- public-project collaborator;
- friendly rival.

A user should ideally form at least one meaningful edge by D14, but social participation remains optional.

## 24. Mentorship system

Veterans can mentor newer users.

Mentor actions:
- answer economic-system questions;
- review a business plan;
- suggest skill paths;
- join cooperative missions.

Rewards:
- mentor reputation;
- badges;
- profile history;
- limited cosmetic/prestige rewards.

Mentors cannot control mentee assets.

## 25. Co-business continuity

Shared companies support:
- asynchronous tasks;
- role-based queues;
- shared weekly objectives;
- partner notifications;
- contribution history.

Avoid designs where one absent player blocks the whole company.

## 26. City identity

Cities provide:
- local goals;
- public projects;
- industry identity;
- city leaderboard based on improvement/contribution;
- community milestones;
- local economic news.

City loyalty should create identity without permanently trapping users.

## 27. Friendly rival system

A rival is a comparable user with similar progression.

Rival cards compare:
- resilience improvement;
- skill growth;
- business margin;
- savings consistency.

Users can mute/remove rivals.

Avoid humiliation or absolute wealth comparison.

## 28. Season continuity

Season design has three layers:
- world story;
- user personal objective;
- community objective.

A season should introduce:
- one macroeconomic variation;
- one new opportunity set;
- one new social/public objective;
- one permanent Career Book record.

## 29. Content cadence

Recommended cadence:
- daily: small world/personal updates;
- weekly: statement + rotating opportunity set;
- 2-week: small event or narrative beat;
- 6–8 week: season;
- quarterly: larger world/system expansion;
- semiannual: major progression layer review.

Cadence is a planning target and should adapt to measured production capacity.

## 30. Endgame / veteran economy

Veterans need horizontal depth:
- advanced business specialization;
- mentorship;
- city governance participation;
- scenario laboratory;
- prestige collections;
- historical records;
- complex cooperative projects.

Do not solve endgame by infinite currency inflation.

## 31. Economic softlock prevention

Detect:
- negative recurring cash flow;
- unaffordable debt service;
- unemployment + low liquidity;
- failed business + no wage income;
- city cost mismatch.

Recovery offers:
- protected basic job;
- restructuring;
- training grant;
- relocation assistance;
- business closure workflow;
- temporary public assistance.

Every user state must have at least one viable recovery path.

## 32. Adaptive difficulty

Difficulty adjusts by:
- skill;
- liquidity;
- experience;
- recent success/failure;
- chosen risk preference.

Never secretly alter already-committed economic outcomes.

Adaptive systems select future offers/challenges, not rewrite past probabilities.

## 33. Personal risk preference

Users can choose:
- conservative;
- balanced;
- growth;
- experimental.

This influences recommendation ranking and optional scenario exposure, not hidden reward multipliers.

## 34. Choice consequence design

Important decisions should have:
- immediate effect;
- delayed effect;
- visible reason;
- reversible or recoverable path where appropriate.

Examples:
- training costs cash now, increases qualification later;
- cheaper city lowers costs but narrows some job options;
- aggressive inventory strategy increases upside and stock risk.

## 35. Surprise and novelty

Novelty pool:
- new job archetype;
- temporary business contract;
- city event;
- policy shift;
- collaborative project;
- skill challenge.

Randomness should alter opportunities, not secretly confiscate accumulated value.

## 36. Collection and prestige

Prestige content:
- career titles;
- company milestones;
- city contribution badges;
- seasonal historical badges;
- office/home cosmetics;
- profile themes;
- non-power collectibles.

Prestige acts as a WLD sink when appropriate but must not create P2W.

## 37. User-generated identity

Users may name:
- company;
- project;
- career goal;
- portfolio strategy label;
- public profile tagline.

UGC requires moderation and privacy controls.

## 38. Story archive

Career Book becomes a long-term archive:
- decisions;
- jobs;
- cities;
- businesses;
- crises;
- recoveries;
- public projects;
- seasons;
- relationships.

Users can browse "1 month ago / 1 year ago" economic history.

## 39. Return triggers and analytics

Instrument event-trigger retention:
- after first salary;
- after first savings goal;
- after first promotion;
- after first business profit;
- after first social connection;
- after first city vote;
- after Time Machine usage;
- after AI advice acceptance/rejection.

Measure D1/D7/D30 return separately for each trigger.

## 40. Retention cohort matrix

Required cohort splits:
- acquisition source;
- country/locale;
- platform;
- first chosen life path;
- social vs solo;
- business vs non-business;
- credit/debt state;
- city;
- season joined;
- notification opt-in;
- AI assistant use;
- returning after inactivity.

Do not optimize using blended retention alone.

## 41. Lifecycle state machine

Suggested lifecycle:
`NEW -> ACTIVATED -> EXPLORING -> ESTABLISHED -> CONNECTED -> INVESTED -> VETERAN`

Dormancy branch:
`ANY_ACTIVE -> AT_RISK -> DORMANT -> RETURNING -> REACTIVATED`.

Lifecycle state is a product segmentation state, not a user quality score.

## 42. Churn-risk model

Signals may include:
- falling session frequency;
- unfinished goals;
- repeated failure;
- cash-flow softlock;
- social partner inactivity;
- feature confusion;
- notification disablement.

Allowed use:
- simplify home;
- suggest recovery;
- recommend new path;
- offer digest;
- surface help.

Prohibited:
- increasing pressure;
- hiding opt-out;
- punitive FOMO;
- targeted exploitative pricing.

## 43. AI retention assistant boundary

AI may determine:
- which explanation is most useful;
- which goal to suggest;
- which recovery path to explain;
- which feature tutorial to surface.

AI may not:
- fabricate urgency;
- threaten loss;
- alter committed outcomes;
- autonomously spend assets;
- target vulnerable users with stronger pressure.

## 44. Onboarding personalization

Ask only a few optional preference questions:
- "What sounds most fun?" career / business / saving / city / social.
- "How much complexity do you want?" simple / guided / advanced.
- "How often do you want reminders?" none / important only / digest.

Behavior can refine recommendations over time.

## 45. Progressive disclosure

Beginner UI:
- Today;
- Money;
- Job;
- Goal.

Intermediate:
- Credit;
- Business;
- City;
- Season.

Advanced:
- statements;
- scenario lab;
- detailed ledger;
- policy/economic indicators.

Avoid showing 18 systems on day one.

## 46. FTUE funnel

Required funnel:
1. account created;
2. starter path selected;
3. first income earned;
4. first obligation paid;
5. first choice made;
6. first goal selected;
7. Career Book entry;
8. first return.

Measure dropoff at every step.

## 47. Failure experience

Failure is content:
- job rejection gives reason and skill path;
- business loss gives diagnosis and recovery;
- loan denial gives transparent factors;
- low credit gives recovery plan;
- season loss gives progress summary.

Failure without next action is prohibited.

## 48. Progress celebration

Celebrate meaningful milestones with:
- animation;
- Career Book entry;
- badge;
- narrative summary;
- optional share card.

Do not interrupt minor actions with excessive reward effects.

## 49. Re-engagement inbox

Instead of many push notifications, in-app inbox groups:
- economic changes;
- social;
- business;
- season;
- public projects.

Users can mark categories as priority or mute them.

## 50. Calendar and planning view

Optional calendar shows:
- payday;
- loan payment;
- business close;
- training completion;
- season event;
- public vote.

No fake event urgency.

## 51. User-controlled pacing

Settings:
- relaxed;
- standard;
- active.

Pacing changes number of optional opportunities and briefing density, not economic fairness.

## 52. Session-length modes

Home supports:
- Quick check: 30 sec–2 min;
- Normal: 5–15 min;
- Deep planning: 20+ min.

Users can intentionally choose session type.

## 53. Ethical retention guardrails

Never use:
- fake countdowns;
- misleading scarcity;
- permanent streak destruction;
- forced ad viewing;
- hidden recurring costs;
- unexpected loss caused only by absence;
- deceptive notification urgency;
- confusing cancellation/opt-out;
- targeted shame;
- dark-pattern purchasing.

## 54. Retention experiments

Every experiment defines:
- hypothesis;
- cohort;
- primary metric;
- guardrail metrics;
- minimum observation window;
- rollback condition.

Primary metrics may include event-based retention, goal completion, feature breadth and social connection.

Guardrails:
- notification opt-out;
- session compulsion;
- support complaints;
- softlocks;
- regret/cancellation;
- accessibility failures.

## 55. A/B test exclusions

Do not A/B test:
- weaker disclosures;
- hidden costs;
- safety limits;
- account security;
- legal notices;
- exploit detection thresholds solely for engagement.

## 58. KPI targets

Targets are hypotheses until observed data exists.

Measure:
- activation;
- D1/D7/D30 retention;
- D90 survival;
- % with one active goal;
- % with one meaningful social edge;
- weekly statement open rate;
- recovery completion;
- reactivation rate;
- average feature breadth;
- healthy session distribution;
- notification satisfaction proxy.

Do not set universal benchmark claims without genre/product evidence.

## 59. SEO-to-retention handoff

Public calculator/user acquisition flow:
`search -> useful tool -> optional save scenario -> signup -> imported simulation -> first personal goal -> life cycle`.

Never force signup before the calculator provides useful output.

## 60. Guest-to-user conversion

Guest can:
- run calculator;
- preview a life path;
- see sample city/economy.

Signup value:
- save results;
- continue history;
- personalize goals;
- join persistent economy.

## 61. Cross-device continuity

Web/app:
- same goal state;
- same Career Book;
- same notifications preferences;
- safe session continuation.

Do not duplicate rewards because of device switching.

## 62. Accessibility and retention

Accessibility failures are retention failures.

Must support:
- keyboard;
- screen reader;
- reduced motion;
- text scaling/reflow;
- high contrast;
- chart text alternatives;
- no time-limited interaction without extension where possible.

## 63. International retention

Do not simply translate Korean events.

Each locale needs:
- culturally understandable economic examples;
- local time/calendar handling;
- localized notification time;
- terminology review;
- content moderation capacity.

Locale and jurisdiction remain separate.

## 64. Operational content tooling

Admin tools need:
- quest template editor;
- opportunity eligibility preview;
- season calendar;
- notification preview and frequency estimate;
- cohort impact preview;
- recovery-flow editor;
- experiment registry.

All high-impact changes are versioned/audited.

## 69. Definition of Done

v527 planning is complete when:
- retention is explicitly designed across first session, D1, D7, D30, D90 and veteran horizons;
- autonomy/competence/relatedness are reflected in mechanics;
- daily/weekly/season/legacy goals are connected;
- dormant recovery and absence-safe economics are defined;
- social, season, narrative and progression loops are specified;
- notification frequency and consent controls exist;
- dark-pattern guardrails are explicit;
- analytics are event/cohort/lifecycle based;
- EN/KO specs are synchronized;
- authority documents and update/work records reference v527;
- no runtime/Test/Production completion is falsely claimed.
