## 1. Gap selected

The largest remaining growth gap is no longer deciding whether Moneyverse should have a Weekly World Brief. It is defining a first edition that can actually prove a return loop without becoming another feature index, patch-note list, finance-promotion surface, or ad container.

Production reality rechecked on 2026-09-13:
- the public home is reachable and already exposes multiple sponsored placements;
- the home still says public monthly/operations news is being prepared;
- the public announcements surface is reachable but still has no published notice;
- the getting-started guide remains broad and foregrounds compound deposits, bonds, loans, virtual stocks, businesses, casino/minigames and a wealth-oriented progression story;
- therefore the pilot brief should reduce cognitive load and establish a different repeated reason to return: curiosity, continuity, learning and personal relevance.

This version narrows the next loop to:

`one world change worth knowing → one explanation → one low-friction continuation → first meaningful action/comeback → D1 continuity → D7 new edition`

## 2. Pilot objective

The first Weekly World Brief should answer four user questions in roughly 1–3 minutes:

1. **What changed?**
2. **Why should I care inside this fictional world?**
3. **What is one useful thing I now understand?**
4. **What is one thing I can do next if I want to continue?**

The pilot is successful only if it creates qualified continuation and return behavior. Page views, ad impressions, long reading time or share count alone are not success.

## 3. First-edition editorial thesis

The first edition should demonstrate the product promise instead of describing every system.

Recommended editorial thesis:

**“Something changed in the Moneyverse this week. Here is the one change that matters, what it teaches about the virtual economy, and one safe way to explore it.”**

The lead story should prefer one of these categories:
- a fictional company/sector change that can be explained without buy/sell language;
- a collection/lore chapter that creates curiosity and long-term identity;
- a profession/world event that can lead to a short first action;
- a season preview that creates anticipation without punishment for arriving late;
- a public-safe community/city project with clear consent and no private-economy exposure.

For the pilot, do **not** lead with:
- casino outcome or jackpot framing;
- loan promotion;
- guaranteed or "stable" yield framing;
- raw WLD wealth growth;
- rankings based primarily on wealth, trading frequency or spending;
- a sponsor message;
- a referral reward.

## 4. Pilot edition structure

### Block A — 15-second orientation

Use one sentence that says what changed and one sentence that reminds readers that WLD/WDX and all economy systems are virtual/game-only.

The first screen should not force readers to parse a feature grid before understanding the story.

### Block B — one lead story

Target reading time: about 45–90 seconds.

Required questions:
- What changed in the fictional world?
- Why is it interesting?
- What uncertainty or trade-off exists?
- What can a newcomer understand without already knowing the full economy?

If a market or company is mentioned, explain the fictional event and uncertainty. Never convert the story into `buy now`, `safe yield`, `undervalued winner`, `recover losses` or similar recommendation language.

### Block C — one learning takeaway

Target reading time: about 20–40 seconds.

Choose one concept tightly related to the story, such as:
- diversification;
- opportunity cost;
- supply and demand;
- scarcity vs artificial scarcity;
- sunk-cost awareness;
- uncertainty and scenario thinking;
- source/sink/transfer distinctions in a game economy;
- budgeting or prioritization.

The learning block should leave value even if the reader never signs in.

### Block D — one contextual continuation

Only one primary continuation should be visually dominant.

Examples:
- `Preview this collection chapter`;
- `See the fictional company background`;
- `Try one safe replay question`;
- `Choose a profession thread to follow`;
- `View the season catch-up`.

Avoid a generic `Start now` button when the page can preserve the user's current intent.

### Block E — next-week promise

Give one specific, non-punitive reason to return in the next edition.

Good: `Next week, we will show how this event changed the city project.`

Bad: `Come back or lose this reward.`

The return promise should be editorial continuity, not a streak threat.

## 5. First 30 seconds, first 3 minutes, first session

### First 30 seconds
The visitor should know:
- what Moneyverse is;
- what changed this week;
- that the economy is fictional/game-only;
- that one useful story can be consumed without signing in.

### First 3 minutes
The visitor should:
- finish the lead story or learning block;
- understand one concept;
- see one contextual next step;
- not be asked to learn banking, stocks, business, jobs, shop and casino simultaneously.

### First session after signup
If the user signs in from the brief, preserve the originating topic. The user should land on the relevant continuation rather than an unrelated generic dashboard whenever product safety permits.

A first meaningful action may be:
- saving/following a fictional-company or collection thread;
- completing one profession or learning action;
- recording one simulated reflection/journal choice;
- beginning one collection or season path;
- choosing one next goal.

Login, wallet view or ad interaction alone is not activation.

## 6. Retention ladder from the pilot

- **D1:** remind the user what they chose or learned, not what they missed.
- **D3:** deepen the same preference with one related story, collection, profession or learning item.
- **D7:** publish the next edition and answer one open question left by the prior edition.
- **D14:** connect the thread to a broader season/world/community change.
- **D30:** show how the user's chosen thread became part of a personal archive, collection, profession identity or longer aspiration.

The editorial principle is **continuity before novelty overload**.

## 7. Habit/session design

The brief must support different session lengths:

- **1–3 minutes:** read the lead story, learn one thing, choose one next action;
- **5–15 minutes:** continue into one meaningful product action or deeper explainer;
- **30+ minutes:** self-directed play, collection curation, profession/business planning, archive/lore exploration or community project participation.

Do not make long sessions structurally mandatory for preserving ordinary progress.

## 8. Acquisition and SEO

The pilot edition may become indexable only if it has standalone value independent of signup and advertising.

SEO path:

`qualified impression → useful read → contextual continuation → signup/comeback → activation → D7 → D30 → retention-adjusted contribution`

Editorial requirements:
- unique human-readable title and summary;
- clear game-only context when financial concepts appear;
- original explanatory value, not keyword aggregation;
- strong internal links only to relevant durable explainers;
- no doorway pages, thin AI variants or automatically generated per-user recap pages;
- private account, balance, portfolio, debt, security, recovery, moderation and admin surfaces remain non-indexable/private.

Use Google Search Console generative-AI reporting as a discovery diagnostic, not a content-production target.

## 9. Viral/share design

The pilot share object should be the story or learning insight, not a referral reward.

A recipient should see:
- what happened;
- why it is interesting;
- that it is part of a fictional virtual economy;
- one optional way to explore more.

Default exclusions from share payloads:
- WLD balance;
- portfolio/holdings/debt;
- casino result;
- account existence or security state;
- hidden friends/club membership;
- session/auth/recovery identifiers.

Referral rewards, if later connected, should remain downstream-retention based and non-P2W.

## 10. Monetization rule for the pilot

The first pilot edition is primarily a retention experiment, not an inventory experiment.

Rules:
- deliver the lead story and learning value before monetization becomes visually dominant;
- do not turn an empty/near-empty edition into an ad page;
- no ad should resemble the contextual continuation CTA;
- no sponsor module adjacent to buy/sell/borrow/repay/casino-like actions;
- sponsor relationships must be clearly labeled and separated from editorial conclusions;
- no sponsor may purchase WDX conclusions, rankings, loan terms, moderation priority or disguised recommendations.

Primary business metric: **retention-adjusted contribution**, not raw eCPM or impressions.

## 11. Funnel and cohorts

Primary pilot funnel:

`home/search/share → pilot brief view → engaged read → contextual continuation → signup/comeback → first meaningful action → D1 → D7 next-edition return → D30`

Required cohorts:
- first-time vs returning anonymous;
- organic vs direct vs social/share vs referral vs paid;
- new signed-in vs established vs lapsed;
- mobile vs desktop;
- Korean vs other locale where applicable;
- content theme;
- exposed vs unexposed to monetization;
- consent/personalization state when lawful and relevant.

## 13. Experiment backlog

### Experiment A — one lead story vs three equal stories
Hypothesis: one editorially chosen lead lowers cognitive load and improves meaningful continuation.
Target: first-time and returning anonymous brief readers.
Control: three equally weighted story cards.
Treatment: one lead story plus secondary links below.
Primary: contextual continuation → meaningful action.
Guardrails: bounce, D1/D7, topic dissatisfaction.
Minimum observation: at least 3 editions plus D7 maturation.
Success: keep one-lead hierarchy. Failure: allow two lead choices, not a full feature grid.

### Experiment B — open-loop next-week promise vs no continuity promise
Hypothesis: a specific editorial question for next week improves D7 return without punitive FOMO.
Primary: next-edition return rate.
Guardrails: notification opt-out, perceived pressure, misleading teaser complaints.
Minimum observation: 4 publication cycles.
Success: keep continuity questions. Failure: use archive-first catch-up instead of teaser language.

### Experiment C — contextual signup CTA vs generic signup CTA
Hypothesis: preserving the current story intent increases signup-to-activation and lowers time-to-first-value.
Primary: signup → meaningful action.
Guardrails: auth abandonment, post-login bounce, errors.
Minimum observation: D7-matured signups.

### Experiment D — editorial learning block vs reward block
Hypothesis: a useful learning takeaway creates higher-quality return behavior than a reward-first module.
Primary: D7/D30 and share → activation quality.
Guardrails: low completion, misleading finance claims, economy abuse.
Minimum observation: multiple topic types.

### Experiment E — value-first ad placement vs early ad placement
Hypothesis: preserving the first useful block improves activation/retention enough to offset lower immediate ad exposure.
Primary: retention-adjusted contribution.
Guardrails: accidental clicks, CWV, bounce, D7/D30, ad complaints.
Minimum observation: sufficient revenue volume plus D7/D30 maturation.

## 14. Security, abuse and privacy review

### High — public/private leakage
User impact: targeted phishing, stalking, embarrassment or account targeting.
Abuse scenario: a share/brief module exposes balance, holdings, debt, hidden relationships, account/security/recovery state or moderation information.
Minimum protection: public-safe fields only, explicit opt-in for personalized public content, redact/hide/delete path, no secret/session/recovery identifiers.
Separate development/QA: required before personalized public/share surfaces.

### High — official-brief impersonation/phishing
User impact: credential theft/account takeover.
Abuse scenario: attackers copy Weekly Brief branding and use fake login/reward links.
Minimum protection: consistent official-domain cues, no balance-loss urgency, no sensitive account details in notification copy, no secret-bearing deep links.
Separate development/QA: required for email, push, deep-link and external social campaigns.

### High — finance-like claim drift
User impact: misunderstanding virtual systems as investment/deposit/guaranteed-return products.
Abuse scenario: editorial copy evolves into safe-yield, undervalued-stock, guaranteed-passive-income or loss-recovery messaging.
Minimum protection: nearby game-only disclosure, editorial review, no investment-advice positioning or real-return guarantee.
Legal/product review: required if cash redemption or real financial characteristics are ever proposed.

### Medium — engagement/referral farming
No meaningful WLD/WDX for views, shares or raw signups. Evaluate referral value only after downstream retained milestones and fraud adjustment.

### Medium — UGC spotlight harm
Require public consent, report/block/takedown, anti-doxxing rules, safe outbound-link handling and no forced real-world identity.

### Medium — analytics/ad overcollection
Do not send tokens, credentials, private economy/security state or unnecessary identifiers to third-party analytics/ad systems. Sensitive profiling and minor/personalized-ad targeting remain separate review gates.

## 15. Legal and trust cautions

- WLD/WDX remain virtual/simulated/game-only.
- Do not imply cash redemption, real securities, deposits, guaranteed income or investment advice.
- Sponsored/native content and compensated creator relationships require clear disclosure.
- Subscription terms must remain clear, affirmative and easy to cancel.
- Personalized advertising, new tracking vendors, minor targeting, high-value referral incentives and public UGC expansion require separate privacy/legal/trust review.
- Do not infer sensitive real-world financial status or risk tolerance from Moneyverse behavior for advertising or eligibility decisions.

## 16. External research note

Research date: 2026-09-13.

### Directly adopted

1. **Spotify Newsroom, 2026-07-10 — weekly discovery and Release Radar controls.**
   - Finding: Spotify maintains predictable weekly discovery surfaces and adds user controls to shape relevance; Release Radar reaches nearly 9 million weekly listeners.
   - Adoption: use a predictable weekly return moment and preserve user choice over which content thread to continue. Do not use Spotify scale as a Moneyverse forecast.

2. **Discord, 2026-08-20 — game discovery and social play tools.**
   - Finding: Discord frames discovery around downstream gameplay events and retention rather than impression volume alone.
   - Adoption: judge the brief by meaningful continuation and retained cohorts, not article views.

3. **Discord, 2026-03-12 — verified official game profiles.**
   - Finding: Discord emphasizes a claimed/verified official destination so players can distinguish trusted game information and links from impersonation.
   - Adoption: maintain consistent official-domain and brand cues for recurring briefs and future notifications; treat impersonation as a product-trust risk.

4. **Google Search Central, 2026-06-03 / worldwide rollout 2026-08-31 — generative-AI performance reports.**
   - Finding: Search Console exposes AI-search/Discover visibility as a reporting layer.
   - Adoption: use AI visibility diagnostically while optimizing for human usefulness and downstream activation/retention.

### Reference / guardrail

5. **Google Search Central, 2026-08-28 — Site Reputation Policy update.**
   - Guardrail: do not turn sponsor/creator modules into third-party SEO inventory that exploits the Moneyverse domain's reputation.

6. **FTC, 2026-01-13 JustAnswer recurring-subscription complaint and 2026-07-06 Hims & Hers privacy/subscription action.**
   - Guardrail: keep material subscription terms conspicuous, obtain affirmative consent, make cancellation straightforward, and avoid leaking sensitive behavioral data to ad platforms.

7. **Korea PIPC, 2026-04-01 COPPA 2.0 international-policy summary.**
   - Reference: youth privacy and personalized advertising remain moving policy areas; minor targeting should stay behind explicit age/privacy/legal review rather than being enabled by default.
