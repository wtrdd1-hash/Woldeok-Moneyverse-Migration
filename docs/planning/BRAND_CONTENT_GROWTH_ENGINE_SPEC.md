## 1. Gap selected

The largest remaining consumer-growth gap is a durable reason to revisit Moneyverse on days when a user does not intend to play a full session.

The current growth stack already defines pre-signup value, D1–D30 return reasons, and a retention-to-viral artifact loop. What is still weak is a repeatable public content cadence that can simultaneously:

- answer why a first-time visitor should care;
- create branded/direct/organic return visits;
- make the fictional world feel alive between play sessions;
- give lapsed users an easy way to understand what changed;
- create season anticipation without loss-aversion pressure;
- reinforce collection, identity, learning, club and city aspirations;
- provide monetizable public inventory only after editorial value is established.

The target loop is:

`useful public content → curiosity about a living virtual world → contextual sample/exploration → signup or comeback → meaningful action → personal progress/artifact → reason to revisit future content`

Content must be valuable even when the visitor never signs up.

## 2. Brand promise

The consumer-facing brand promise for this content system is:

**“A living virtual economy where you can learn, build, collect, and leave a history.”**

Editorial tone should emphasize curiosity, agency, learning, identity, world-building and reflection.

Avoid positioning based on:
- getting rich;
- beating a real market;
- guaranteed returns;
- fear of missing rewards;
- gambling-style excitement;
- wealth as the only status signal.

Whenever WLD, WDX, banking or market concepts appear publicly, the experience must preserve the existing meaning that they are virtual/simulated/game-only and are not real securities, deposits, fiat money or promised investment returns.

## 3. Content portfolio

### 3.1 Weekly Moneyverse World Brief

Purpose: become the steady recurring reason to check what is new.

Each edition should contain roughly 3–5 meaningful items such as:
- fictional-company or sector developments;
- season/world changes;
- a new collection/lore thread;
- a club/city/community project highlight;
- one short learning takeaway;
- one optional next action.

The brief should be useful in a 1–3 minute session. It should not become a checklist or imply that the reader has lost value by arriving late.

Preferred CTA style:
- “Explore this virtual company.”
- “Try the simulated scenario.”
- “See the collection story.”
- “Catch up on this season.”

Avoid generic top-of-page signup pressure before the reader receives value.

### 3.2 Virtual Company & Market Stories

Purpose: turn the fictional issuer universe into understandable recurring stories rather than price tables alone.

A story should explain:
- what changed in the fictional company/world;
- why it matters inside Moneyverse;
- what uncertainty or trade-off exists;
- a learning/reflection question;
- one relevant simulated experience.

Do not publish “buy/sell now,” “guaranteed winner,” or recovery-trading language. Profit-only leaderboards and trading-frequency challenges are not editorial goals.

### 3.3 Money Skills Lab

Purpose: create useful search/discovery content that also improves the quality of later gameplay.

Priority topics:
- diversification and concentration;
- risk vs reward;
- loss review and journaling;
- budgeting and opportunity cost;
- supply/demand and fictional market events;
- how fees/sinks/transfers differ in a virtual economy;
- how to compare choices without predicting guaranteed outcomes.

Default format: a 3–5 minute explanation plus an optional simulated example. Learning completion is more important than trade volume.

### 3.4 Season Anticipation & Archive

Purpose: make the world feel continuous before, during and after a season.

Use the existing D-14/D-7/D-3/D-1 preview concept, but each touchpoint must add genuinely new context rather than repeat a countdown.

Example progression:
- D-14: theme and world premise;
- D-7: fictional company/job/business/collection preview;
- D-3: community/city/club opportunities and catch-up explanation;
- D-1: concise “what starts tomorrow” guide;
- launch: clear entry paths for new, returning and active users;
- post-season: permanent archive, retrospective, Hall of Fame and collectible history.

No “log in now or lose everything” framing. Mid-season entrants and returning users must have a readable catch-up route.

### 3.5 Collection & Lore Spotlight

Purpose: strengthen long-term aspiration and voluntary sinks through meaning rather than forced taxation.

Spotlight stories can connect:
- collection sets;
- restoration and engraving;
- rooms/offices/headquarters;
- professions/business identity;
- museum/archive prestige;
- city/landmark sponsorship.

The editorial question is “why would I want this and what story does it let me express?” rather than “how can the system remove more WLD?”

### 3.6 Community / Club / City Spotlight

Purpose: show that the world contains other people and persistent community achievements.

Eligible stories should be opt-in, public-safe and useful without exposing private social or asset data.

Good candidates:
- completed club/city projects;
- creative public spaces;
- educational retrospectives;
- collection or community milestones;
- moderated creator/community challenges.

Do not publish private contact information, hidden membership data, private balances/portfolios, moderation status or inferred sensitive traits.

## 4. Cadence without content debt

The baseline cadence is intentionally sustainable:

- one Weekly World Brief as the recurring anchor;
- one or two deeper stories/learning pieces during a normal week when quality justifies them;
- season preview cadence around actual season transitions;
- permanent archives for stories with durable value;
- no requirement to publish filler every day.

A missed publishing day must not damage user progression. Editorial cadence is a discovery/retention tool, not a streak users must obey.

Quality is prioritized over URL volume.

## 5. Lifecycle mapping

### First visitor
Answer within the first screen:
- what Moneyverse is;
- why this page is useful now;
- what is fictional/simulated;
- one related experience they can preview without account pressure.

### Newly activated / D1
Show one short piece related to the path the user already chose. The content should reinforce the next goal, not open five new systems.

### D3
Use one story continuation, collection/lore explanation or beginner lesson to help the user form preferences and identity.

### D7
The Weekly World Brief becomes the anchor: “what changed in the world + what changed for me.” Personal context should stay private unless the user explicitly chooses to share it.

### D14
Use deeper season, fictional-company, club/city or collection stories to show that the world has continuity beyond a single checklist.

### D30+
Connect personal archive and world archive. Long-term users should be able to remember “what the world was like when I joined” and “what I built during that period.”

### Lapsed users
A comeback editorial should summarize meaningful change without scolding the user or advertising losses. Preferred framing:

“Here is what changed while you were away, what is still yours, and one easy place to restart.”

## 6. Content-to-product funnel

Primary funnel:

`search/social/direct/share → useful content landing → engaged value → contextual exploration/sample → signup or comeback → first meaningful action → D1 → D7 → D30`

Secondary retention loop:

`active/returning user → recurring brief/story → meaningful action → personal progress → archive/share artifact → later content revisit`

A page is not successful because it ranks or receives impressions. It is successful if it creates useful understanding and qualified downstream behavior.

## 7. SEO strategy

### Priority search clusters

1. beginner simulated-economy / finance-learning questions;
2. virtual-company and fictional-market explainers;
3. season preview, launch guide and archive;
4. glossary/explainer topics with real educational value;
5. collection/lore and world-building pages;
6. substantial opt-in community project/showcase stories.

### Content-quality rules

- each indexable page must satisfy an identifiable reader intent without requiring signup;
- EN and KO versions must each read naturally and provide standalone value;
- do not mass-produce thin issuer/profile/recap pages merely to expand indexed URL count;
- no keyword stuffing, cloaking, doorway pages or misleading functionality;
- editorial/sponsor/UGC origin must be understandable;
- update dates only when meaningful content was actually updated;
- private account, portfolio, balance, billing, security, recovery, moderation and internal risk pages remain outside public search.

Organic KPI path:

`organic impression → qualified click → engaged read → contextual sample → signup/comeback → activation → D7/D30 → contribution margin`

Also track branded-query/direct-return growth so content is not judged only as one-time search acquisition.

## 8. Viral and social connection

Public editorial can be shared on its own. Personal artifacts remain opt-in and follow the `RETENTION_TO_VIRAL_GROWTH_LOOP_SPEC`.

Preferred share themes:
- a useful learning insight;
- a fictional-company/world story;
- a collection/lore discovery;
- a season chapter;
- a club/city achievement;
- an owner-first progress story.

Do not make balance, profit, debt, casino outcome or private portfolio the default social card.

Recipient pages must remain understandable without signup. Shared URLs/cards must not contain session secrets, account recovery data, private asset details or hidden social identifiers.

## 9. Monetization after editorial value

Good fits after the reader has received value:
- clearly labeled contextual display/native sponsorship;
- ad-free subscription;
- non-P2W profile/space/archive/collection presentation products;
- sponsor-supported educational or lore series with editorial separation;
- B2B/B2B2C support for non-competitive community/city projects where the commercial relationship is clear.

Do not allow a sponsor to purchase:
- favorable WDX price movement or editorial conclusion;
- rank/league advantage;
- loan/banking advantage;
- moderation priority;
- disguised organic endorsement;
- a placement that imitates buy/sell/loan/repay controls.

Revenue evaluation must include D7/D30 retention, ad-induced churn, subscription cancellation, content production cost, support/moderation cost and contribution margin.

## 10. KPI framework

### Discovery
- organic engaged visits by content pillar;
- branded organic/direct returning share;
- new vs returning content readers;
- content revisit within 7/30 days;
- search query cluster → engaged visit.

### Activation
- content → contextual sample/exploration;
- content-assisted signup;
- content-assisted activation;
- time from content entry to first meaningful action;
- first-session completion by content source.

### Retention
- D1/D3/D7/D14/D30 by content-assisted cohort;
- Weekly World Brief reader → meaningful action;
- content-reader → comeback rate;
- active-player → recurring content use;
- returning-user share and WAU/MAU by content exposure cohort.

### Viral
- content/artifact share → engaged visit;
- share → pre-signup value → activation → D7;
- report/hide/takedown rate for public community content.

### Revenue
- ARPU/ARPDAU by content cohort;
- sponsor/ad revenue per engaged content user;
- subscription conversion after repeated value;
- ad-induced churn;
- LTV/CAC and contribution margin by acquisition/content source.

### Trust guardrails
- fake-signup rate;
- referral/engagement fraud rate;
- account-takeover signal rate;
- spam/report rate;
- privacy complaint rate;
- suspicious duplicate-reward rate;
- sponsor/creator disclosure complaints;
- community takedown/escalation rate.

## 11. Experiment backlog

### A. Contextual CTA in Weekly World Brief
Hypothesis: a CTA tied to the story creates more qualified product action than a generic “Play/Sign up” CTA.
Target cohort: anonymous and returning readers of the brief.
Entry point: end of the first meaningful story block.
Control: generic product CTA.
Treatment: story-specific simulated exploration CTA.
Primary metric: content → meaningful action/signup/comeback conversion.
Guardrails: bounce, D7, privacy/spam complaints, fake signup.
Minimum observation: at least two complete weekly cycles plus D7 maturation and adequate cohort size.
Success: expand contextual mapping to the best-performing pillars.
Failure: reduce CTA complexity and retain only high-intent contexts.

### B. 30-second simulated sample before signup
Hypothesis: useful pre-signup interaction improves activation quality from high-intent educational/search pages.
Target cohort: first-time organic visitors on beginner/market-learning content.
Entry point: after the core explanation.
Control: direct signup CTA.
Treatment: short non-authoritative sample followed by save/start CTA.
Primary metric: activation rate and time-to-first-value.
Guardrails: fake signup, error rate, privacy complaint, D7.
Minimum observation: enough conversions for stable activation comparison plus full D7 maturation.
Success: extend to the highest-intent content clusters.
Failure: keep educational content standalone and remove low-value interaction.

### C. Season preview ladder vs single launch announcement
Hypothesis: genuinely additive D-14/D-7/D-3/D-1 context improves return/activation without FOMO harm.
Target cohort: active, returning and recently lapsed users eligible for season content.
Entry point: public season/editorial surfaces.
Control: one launch announcement.
Treatment: progressive preview ladder.
Primary metric: season return/reactivation and first meaningful season action.
Guardrails: notification opt-out, report rate, user-pressure signal, D7/D30.
Minimum observation: one full preview-to-launch window and a mid-season catch-up cohort.
Success: retain the ladder and refine content depth.
Failure: reduce touchpoints while preserving a durable archive/catch-up guide.

### D. Opt-in community spotlight
Hypothesis: staff-curated, consented community stories create qualified exploration without the trust cost of open promotion inventory.
Target cohort: readers of club/city/community content.
Entry point: community spotlight page/module.
Control: generic project promo.
Treatment: opt-in narrative spotlight with context and public-safe showcase.
Primary metric: club/project exploration and meaningful participation.
Guardrails: harassment, doxxing/privacy complaints, reports/takedowns.
Minimum observation: enough spotlights to observe moderation/report patterns, not one featured story.
Success: expand carefully with editorial standards.
Failure: restrict to staff-authored/public-safe project summaries.

### E. Sponsor placement after value vs top-of-page
Hypothesis: delaying sponsorship until after the first useful content block preserves retention and trust while maintaining sustainable yield.
Target cohort: eligible public-content readers.
Entry point: monetizable public guide/editorial page.
Control: early/top sponsorship insertion.
Treatment: sponsorship after first useful section.
Primary metric: revenue per engaged content session and sponsor yield.
Guardrails: bounce, content completion, D7/D30, ad-induced churn, accidental click/trust complaint.
Minimum observation: enough eligible sessions to compare both revenue and downstream retention.
Success: standardize value-first placement.
Failure: if early placement raises short-term revenue but harms retention/trust, reject it; otherwise iterate format.

## 12. Security, abuse and privacy review

### High — public/private boundary leakage
User impact: private balances, positions, account existence, social relationships or security context can enable embarrassment, stalking, phishing or targeted account attacks.
Abuse scenario: a content/community template accidentally publishes personalized or internal fields.
Minimum protection: explicit public scope/opt-in; public-safe fields only; never publish private balances/portfolio/security/recovery/moderation/risk data; clear hide/remove/redact path.
Separate development/QA required: yes before any new personalized public surface ships.

### High — community spotlight / UGC impersonation, phishing or doxxing
User impact: harassment, malicious links, false identity claims and leakage of real-world contact data.
Minimum protection: explicit participant consent, moderation/report/takedown path, no forced real identity, safe outbound-link policy, no raw private contact details.
Separate development/QA required: yes before scaling user-submitted spotlight content.

### High — sponsor/creator deception
User impact: users mistake paid promotion for neutral Moneyverse analysis, including in financial-like fictional-market contexts.
Minimum protection: clear local-language ad/sponsor/material-connection disclosure near the content; editorial conclusion remains independent; no paid “signal” or guaranteed-return language.
Separate development/QA required: product/legal/trust review before sponsor workflow launch.

### Medium — bot/SEO/referral engagement fraud
User impact: polluted acquisition metrics, higher CAC and possible reward abuse.
Minimum protection: no meaningful WLD/WDX reward for page views, shares or raw signup; use fraud-adjusted attribution; treat unusual engagement as diagnostic rather than value creation.
Separate development/QA required: yes if economic rewards or paid acquisition are attached to these signals.

### Medium — analytics/ad privacy overcollection
Minimum protection: purpose-limited analytics; no tokens/credentials/private financial-game fields in third-party analytics; personalized advertising only after age/region/privacy review; conservative handling of minors.

### Medium — phishing/ATO pressure through comeback content
Minimum protection: no balance/loss threats, secret-bearing links or sensitive asset details in notifications/share text; official destinations must be recognizable; deep-link/reengagement implementation requires security QA.

Existing authentication, session, RBAC, ledger, administrator and privacy boundaries remain unchanged by this consumer-growth plan.

## 13. Legal / policy cautions

- WLD/WDX remain virtual/simulated/game-only in public editorial and marketing.
- Do not present fictional-company stories as real investment recommendations or guaranteed-return claims.
- Sponsored/native content and compensated creator relationships require clear disclosure appropriate to Korea/U.S. context.
- Community spotlights require privacy/publication consent appropriate to what is shown.
- Personalized advertising, new tracking vendors, child/minor targeting, real-money referral value or materially expanded UGC require separate privacy/legal/trust review before launch.
- Any future change toward real securities, deposits, cash redemption or real financial products is outside this specification and requires separate legal/product review.

   - Reinforces that trusted-domain reputation must not be exploited by third-party content intended primarily to manipulate rankings.

5. FTC Native Advertising guidance, re-checked 2026-09-13.
   - Necessary advertising disclosures must be clear, prominent and understandable near the sponsored content.

   - Social commitment can improve repeat participation, but the mechanism relies on daily obligation.
   - Used only as evidence that social context can reinforce habit. Moneyverse does not adopt punitive shared streak loss or friend-pressure as a default retention mechanic.
