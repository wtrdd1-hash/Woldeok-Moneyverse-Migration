## 1. Gap selected

The largest remaining consumer-growth gap is not another referral reward or another feature list. Moneyverse already describes community as part of the product, but a first-time or logged-out visitor has weak proof that there is a living human world worth joining.

Current public reality shows:
- the home describes Moneyverse as a Discord-connected community virtual economy;
- the public lobby explains that visitors can greet people who are online, but the visible state can be empty and posting requires login;
- Monthly Notes and `/announcements` are still empty;
- sponsored inventory is already present on public surfaces.

The growth problem is therefore:

`public discovery → credible human proof → safe low-friction observation → one contextual participation path → signup only when needed → first contribution/value → D1/D7 relationship`

The product should make people feel that real people are building, learning, collecting and reacting here without fabricating activity, exposing private data or forcing users directly into chat.

## 2. Consumer promise

**“See what people are genuinely doing here before deciding whether you want to join.”**

Community proof must be truthful, understandable without an account, useful even when current live activity is low, and safe for both the people being represented and the visitor.

Do not use fake online counts, fabricated testimonials, artificial chat messages, false urgency or anonymous financial-performance claims to simulate popularity.

## 3. Community proof ladder

Use four layers, from safest/lowest-friction to highest-interaction.

### Layer A — editorial world proof
Public, staff-reviewed material that demonstrates a living product:
- Weekly World Brief;
- season/world change summaries;
- fictional-company/lore updates;
- collection or profession explainers;
- community project retrospectives.

This is the default proof layer when live community activity is sparse.

### Layer B — aggregated public-safe activity
Examples:
- “3 collection chapters completed this week”;
- “2 public community projects are active”;
- “this week’s most explored profession: logistics,” only when the sample is sufficient and non-sensitive;
- public-safe event participation totals.

Never expose precise private financial-game activity, hidden cohorts, security/fraud labels, private social graphs or small-n group data that can identify people.

### Layer C — opt-in member artifacts
Examples:
- reviewed collection/space showcase;
- season history;
- learning replay;
- profession milestone;
- community contribution story.

The owner must understand what becomes public. Private achievement must remain intact even if the public artifact is disabled later.

### Layer D — live participation
Examples:
- lobby greeting;
- public community-board participation;
- club/project participation after the user understands context.

Live interaction is not the first mandatory step. A visitor should be able to observe value before being asked to speak.

## 4. First 30 seconds, first 3 minutes, first session

### First 30 seconds
The visitor should understand:
1. Moneyverse is a game-only virtual economy/community;
2. real people create progress/history here;
3. one concrete public-safe example is visible now;
4. they can look around before signing up.

Primary visual hierarchy:
- one real current story or reviewed artifact;
- plain-language context;
- one exploration CTA;
- secondary login CTA.

Do not lead with a dense grid of wallet, loan, stock, casino and shop systems as equal acquisition messages.

### First 3 minutes
Offer one of three low-risk exploration paths:
- **See what changed** — Weekly World Brief/season story;
- **See what people made** — reviewed public showcase/project;
- **Try one sample** — collection/profession/learning preview.

Each path ends with one contextual next action, not a generic wall of links.

### First session
A successful first session produces at least one of:
- one interest thread saved;
- one sample/learning action completed;
- one public-safe project/collection explored deeply enough to choose a path;
- one first contribution after authentication.

Login alone, opening the lobby or clicking an ad is not activation.

## 5. Spectator-to-participant funnel

Canonical funnel:

`search/share/home → public human/world proof → engaged observation → contextual preview → signup/auth only when necessary → first meaningful contribution/action → D1 continuation → D7 relationship`

### Signup rationale
The signup promise should preserve the visitor’s current interest:
- “save this collection path”;
- “follow this world/project update”;
- “start your own version”;
- “join this discussion/project.”

Avoid “create an account to see more” when the service can safely demonstrate value first.

### D1
Return to the exact interest or contribution the user chose. The purpose is recognition and continuity.

### D3
Show one adjacent person/world artifact or related path, not an undifferentiated activity feed.

### D7
Show what changed in the chosen community/world thread and one meaningful way the user can continue.

### D14/D30+
Connect repeated participation to identity, archive, collection, club/city project, season history or profession mastery rather than raw wealth.

## 6. Designing for low activity honestly

Early-stage community products must not pretend to be busy.

When the live lobby is empty:
- say it is quiet rather than fabricating presence;
- provide a reviewed “what people have been doing” archive;
- show the next scheduled/public community moment if one genuinely exists;
- offer a useful solo action that can later become social;
- let users leave a durable, moderated contribution only where the existing community/content model supports it.

Preferred empty-state principle:
**quiet now ≠ no value**.

The product should convert temporary emptiness into asynchronous evidence, not fake concurrency.

## 7. Acquisition channels

### Search
Priority public entry pages:
- substantial season/world archives;
- beginner learning guides;
- fictional-company/lore explainers;
- reviewed community project retrospectives;
- public collection/profession showcases with real context.

Do not mass-index thin user cards, empty lobby states, referral claim pages or auto-generated “people are active” pages.

### Share/viral
Best share units are artifacts with independent meaning:
- completed collection/story;
- learning insight;
- season chapter;
- public project contribution;
- space/showcase.

Recipient flow must provide context before authentication.

### Creator/community partnerships
Use creators to explain, curate or host real activities. Do not manufacture fake consensus or reward positive financial-style claims.

### Paid acquisition
Judge paid traffic by `visit → engaged proof → signup → activation → D7/D30 → LTV`, not impressions or raw installs. Fraud-adjusted CAC must include fake signup, referral abuse and low-quality incentive traffic.

## 8. Social proof rules

Allowed proof:
- truthful staff-reviewed stories;
- opt-in member artifacts;
- sufficiently aggregated public-safe activity;
- clearly dated community/world updates;
- verified/official service identity where important.

Disallowed proof:
- fake users/messages;
- inflated online/member counts;
- wealth/profit claims used as endorsements;
- screenshots exposing private balances, debt, holdings or moderation state;
- paid promotion presented as organic opinion;
- testimonials conditioned on positive sentiment.

## 9. Community safety and trust

### High — harassment, impersonation and doxxing
User impact: intimidation, exposure of real identity/location/contact data, unsafe off-platform migration.
Abuse scenario: a public showcase or lobby interaction is used to identify, harass or impersonate a member.
Minimum protection: public identity must be chosen/limited; clear block/report paths where persistent interaction exists; no forced real-name display; remove precise contact/location/security data; reviewed public artifacts before broad discovery where appropriate.
Separate development/QA required: yes before expanding public UGC/community discovery.

### High — phishing and malicious links
User impact: account takeover, credential theft, malware exposure.
Abuse scenario: attackers imitate official Moneyverse project/event messages or post malicious external URLs.
Minimum protection: clear official-domain/official-account signals; external-link safety policy; no credentials/auth codes in community content; no “asset loss” urgency; suspicious links/content must be reportable/removable.
Separate development/QA required: yes for persistent UGC, external links, notifications or deep links.

### High — public/private boundary leakage
User impact: targeted fraud, embarrassment, stalking.
Abuse scenario: community proof exposes WLD balances, WDX holdings, debt, private friend/club graph, account state, recovery/security state or fraud flags.
Minimum protection: explicit public-safe allowlist; personalized financial/security/social data private by default; small-n aggregation suppression; indexing limited to intentionally public content.
Separate development/QA required: yes before personalized public proof surfaces.

### High — bot/spam/astroturfed community proof
User impact: misleading trust, degraded community, referral/ad fraud.
Abuse scenario: multi-account bots create fake activity, endorsements or public artifacts to look popular or earn rewards.
Minimum protection: no meaningful WLD/WDX reward for raw posts/views/invites; rate/eligibility controls when implemented; moderation/reporting; separate organic proof from sponsored/creator material; fraud-adjusted metrics.
Separate development/QA required: yes before any incentive tied to public participation.

### Medium — minors and age-sensitive interaction
Current operating terms require age 14+. Public community growth must not use targeted adult/financial-like messaging to minors, infer age-sensitive traits unnecessarily or expand cross-user interaction without the applicable age/privacy/safety review.

## 10. SEO/content policy

Community SEO should be based on substantial human/world context, not index volume.

Index candidates:
- curated project retrospectives;
- season/world archives;
- high-context opt-in showcase pages;
- original educational/community explainers.

Usually private/noindex:
- live lobby transcripts/state;
- thin profile cards;
- private/semi-private shares;
- raw activity feeds;
- balances/portfolio/debt;
- moderation/recovery/security pages;
- referral reward/claim pages.

Google’s user-generated-spam guidance supports clear abuse policy, reporting and spam-account controls for platforms. Naver’s current guidance emphasizes user value, clear index/non-index boundaries and avoidance of low-quality mass-generated or misleading pages.

## 11. Monetization

Community proof must not become an ad wrapper.

Order of operations:
1. show the human/world value;
2. let the visitor understand or explore it;
3. preserve the next participation action;
4. only then place reviewed monetization.

Do not place ads so they look like community posts or project CTAs. Sponsored/creator content must be labeled clearly. Do not allow payment to buy neutral-looking market conclusions, ranking, moderation privilege, loan terms, WDX advantage or artificial social proof.

Ad-removal subscriptions and non-P2W profile/space/archive expression are better fits after repeated community value is established.

## 12. KPI framework

### Acquisition
- public-proof landing engaged-visit rate;
- organic/share visit → engaged observation;
- creator/community campaign → activation → D7/D30;
- fraud-adjusted CAC by channel.

### Activation
- time to first community/world understanding;
- public proof → contextual preview rate;
- preview → signup rate;
- signup → first meaningful action/contribution;
- first-session completion.

### Retention
- D1 return to chosen thread;
- D3 adjacent-interest continuation;
- D7 community/world-thread return;
- D14/D30 retained participation;
- returning-user share;
- meaningful actions/session.

### Viral
- artifact share rate;
- recipient engaged-read rate;
- share → preview → signup → activation → D7;
- public showcase opt-in rate;
- report/block rate per public artifact/interaction.

### Trust guardrails
- spam/report rate;
- harassment/impersonation/doxxing reports;
- malicious-link reports;
- privacy complaints;
- accidental private-data publication;
- fake-signup/multi-account/referral-fraud rate;
- account-takeover/phishing signals;
- suspicious reward duplication;
- ad-induced churn/accidental-ad-click rate.

## 13. Experiment backlog

### Experiment A — human proof vs feature grid
Hypothesis: a truthful current story/artifact before the feature grid improves qualified activation.
Cohort: first-time anonymous visitors.
Control: current feature-oriented home path.
Treatment: one current reviewed story/artifact + one exploration CTA.
Primary: engaged proof → signup → activation.
Guardrails: bounce, finance-like misunderstanding, privacy complaints, page performance.
Observation: at least one mature D7 cohort and adequate sample.
Next: retain only if activation and D7 improve without trust regression.

### Experiment B — quiet-lobby asynchronous proof
Hypothesis: when no live conversation is visible, a reviewed archive/project story prevents dead-community perception better than a bare empty state.
Cohort: visitors seeing an empty lobby.
Control: empty-state text only.
Treatment: honest quiet-state + one reviewed recent public-safe artifact/project + solo next action.
Primary: lobby → meaningful exploration/activation.
Guardrails: fabricated-activity perception complaints, spam, report rate.
Observation: minimum two weekly cycles.

### Experiment C — observe-before-signup vs auth-first community CTA
Hypothesis: allowing safe observation before authentication raises signup quality.
Cohort: non-member community/referral/share visitors.
Control: login-first CTA.
Treatment: public context/sample then save/join CTA.
Primary: activated signup and D7.
Guardrails: abuse, scraper/spam pressure, privacy exposure.

### Experiment D — contribution identity vs wealth status
Hypothesis: showcasing collection/learning/project contribution creates healthier sharing and retention than wealth/profit-oriented status.
Cohort: D7+ retained users.
Control: economy-stat-forward recap where currently applicable.
Treatment: contribution/identity/history artifact.
Primary: voluntary share → activated recipient → D7.
Guardrails: harassment, comparison pressure, finance-like claims.

### Experiment E — value-before-ad on community proof
Hypothesis: postponing ad exposure until after useful human/world proof improves retention-adjusted contribution.
Cohort: anonymous public-content visitors.
Control: earlier reviewed placement.
Treatment: first proof/next action visually precedes monetization.
Primary: retention-adjusted contribution.
Guardrails: activation, D7/D30, accidental clicks, Core Web Vitals.

## 15. Runtime Product Reality Audit — 2026-09-13

Non-destructive public verification found:
- home is reachable and clearly states that WLD/rewards are game-only virtual data;
- home positions Moneyverse as a Discord-connected community economy;
- a Community Lobby entry exists and explains that messages are not stored server-side;
- the lobby warns against posting passwords, auth codes, financial information, addresses or contact details;
- the current public lobby can visibly appear quiet/empty and requires login to send a message;
- Monthly Notes and `/announcements` remain empty;
- sponsored placements already exist on public surfaces;
- current terms prohibit phishing, impersonation, malicious code, automation abuse and harmful content;
- current privacy policy says lobby messages are not stored, ads are restricted to reviewed public surfaces, and economic activity/balances/trading/preferences are not provided for ad targeting.

Conclusion: runtime verification is available. The immediate growth opportunity is not “add more social features,” but to provide truthful asynchronous proof and one safe path from observation to participation while preserving current privacy/security boundaries.

## 16. Next priority

Validate the narrow loop:

`public proof → 30–90 second observation → one contextual action → signup if needed → first contribution/value → D1 → D7`

Do not expand public UGC, economic referral rewards, notification volume, ad inventory or personalized public activity feeds until this loop shows better activation/D7 with acceptable spam, abuse, privacy and safety guardrails.
