## 1. Gap selected

The current planning stack is strong on feature breadth, pre-signup value proof, seasons, notifications and individual progression systems. The largest consumer-growth gap is that the reasons to return after activation are still described mostly as features rather than as a staged relationship.

Moneyverse needs a clear answer to a different question at each retention horizon:

- D1: did anything I started become meaningfully different?
- D3: am I beginning to build an identity and preference?
- D7: can I look back and feel visible progress?
- D14: am I part of a world, collection or group larger than one session?
- D30: do I have history, status and unfinished aspirations worth preserving?

The objective is not to force daily attendance. It is to make absence survivable and return rewarding.

## 2. Retention promise

The product promise after activation is:

**“Every time you return, you should understand what changed, what you accomplished, and what you can shape next.”**

Retention must come from three forms of continuity:

1. **Personal continuity** — progress, collections, journal/history, preferences and spaces.
2. **World continuity** — fictional companies, markets, businesses, city/community projects and seasons evolve.
3. **Social continuity** — friends, clubs and community create reactions and shared memories without making participation mandatory.

No layer should rely on threatened loss, punitive streak resets or deliberately confusing scarcity.

## 3. D1 → D30 return ladder

### D1 — “Something remembers me”

Primary return reason:
- see one meaningful change related to the first-session choice;
- complete a 1–3 minute follow-up action;
- receive a concise explanation of the next milestone.

Examples by first-session intent:
- Market: one watchlist change + one short learning prompt, not a trading-pressure alert.
- Build: job/profession progress summary + next skill milestone.
- Collect: set progress + one discoverable related item or lore entry.

Home should prioritize one continuation card, not a generic dashboard full of systems.

Success signal: returning user reaches a meaningful continuation action quickly and can state the next goal.

### D3 — “I have a style”

Primary return reason:
- offer a small choice that starts distinguishing the player from everyone else;
- connect two systems without requiring mastery of both.

Examples:
- choose a profession direction;
- choose one collection theme;
- create a simple market thesis/watchlist note;
- personalize profile/space with earned non-P2W expression.

The point of D3 is commitment through preference, not sunk-cost pressure.

### D7 — “I can see a week of progress”

Primary return reason:
- weekly recap showing progress across learning, collection, profession, business/community and season participation;
- one celebration artifact that can optionally be shared;
- one next-week recommendation based on chosen interests.

The weekly recap should compare the player mainly with their own earlier state. Competitive rank can be secondary and opt-in.

A missed day does not break the recap. Seven-day value should be measured by accumulated meaningful actions, not consecutive logins.

### D14 — “The world has moved while I was here”

Primary return reason:
- reveal a medium-horizon change: season preview, fictional issuer story, club/city project milestone, collection chapter, business expansion opportunity or educational replay.
- introduce collaborative value without demanding membership in a club.

At this point the home experience may surface “because you did X, you may care about Y” recommendations, while avoiding sensitive profiling or manipulative targeting.

### D30 — “I have history here”

Primary return reason:
- monthly personal archive: first/most meaningful achievements, collection progress, profession identity, learning highlights and selected social moments;
- visible unfinished aspiration ladder;
- prestige goals that cannot be completed only by spending money.

A D30 user should have durable evidence of history even if their WLD balance is not high.

Long-term retention should increasingly shift from raw accumulation to identity, mastery, collection completeness, spaces, archives, clubs/city contributions, titles and season history.

## 4. Session-length design

### Quick check — 1 to 3 minutes

Purpose: understand change and make one meaningful choice.

Good actions:
- review one watchlist/company event;
- claim or continue one progress milestone;
- add one collection target;
- react to one club/project update;
- review a short weekly/monthly insight.

Avoid turning this into mandatory chores.

### Meaningful session — 5 to 15 minutes

Purpose: make tangible progress.

Good actions:
- complete a job chain;
- review and document a virtual-market decision;
- work toward a collection/set;
- make a business or space improvement;
- participate in a club/city project;
- complete a learning/replay challenge.

### Deep session — 30+ minutes

Purpose: self-directed creation, mastery or social play.

Good actions:
- reorganize/personalize spaces and collections;
- review longer market/business history;
- plan multi-step profession/business goals;
- participate in community projects or club events;
- explore season archives and long-form educational content.

The same account should support all three modes without penalizing players who prefer short sessions.

## 5. Home by lifecycle state

### New / D0–D3
Show:
- one continuation action;
- one progress signal;
- one optional exploration path.

Do not show every major system at once.

### Establishing / D4–D14
Show:
- personal next goal;
- what changed in the world;
- one social or collection opportunity;
- weekly recap when available.

### Established / D15+
Show:
- aspiration ladder progress;
- season/community changes;
- collection/business/space goals;
- self-comparison and archive highlights.

### Returning after inactivity
The first screen should answer:
1. what changed;
2. what is still safe/intact;
3. what can I do now in under five minutes.

Do not lead with “you missed X rewards.” Prefer “here is what changed while you were away.”

## 6. Comeback journey

### 3–6 days inactive
Use a lightweight recap and one continuation action. No large churn bonus.

### 7–13 days inactive
Offer a catch-up choice tied to the player’s prior interest: market-learning recap, profession restart, collection discovery, or season overview.

### 14–29 days inactive
Provide a “return briefing” that compresses missed world changes and suggests one low-friction re-entry objective.

### 30+ days inactive
Treat the user as returning, not failed. Preserve history, explain major product/season changes, and offer a fresh path without forcing the old unfinished checklist.

Catch-up should reduce comprehension debt more than it gives economic value.

## 7. Long-term aspiration ladder

### New player aspiration
“I want to understand this world and make my first choices.”

Value targets:
- first profession direction;
- first collection set;
- first saved market idea;
- first personalized profile/space.

### Mid-stage aspiration
“I want my account to look and feel distinct.”

Value targets:
- specialized profession identity;
- deeper collection themes;
- business/space expansion;
- club/community participation;
- visible season history.

### High-asset aspiration
“I want to turn wealth into expression and legacy.”

Priorities:
- restoration/engraving/provenance presentation;
- premium spaces and headquarters;
- museum/archive completion;
- city/community sponsorship;
- landmark and prestige projects;
- voluntary honor auctions and patronage.

Prefer voluntary identity/prestige sinks over punitive wealth removal.

### Prestige aspiration
“I want a record that proves what I did, not only how much I own.”

Priorities:
- season archive;
- hall of fame;
- rare but non-P2W collection completion;
- mentorship/community recognition;
- permanent records of major projects.

## 8. Viral and social retention loop

A share-worthy output should be understandable without an account and should answer “why is this interesting?” before asking the recipient to sign up.

Priority artifacts:
- weekly progress story;
- completed collection/set card;
- room/office/clubhouse showcase;
- season history card;
- fictional-company thesis/replay card;
- club/city project completion;
- learning milestone.

Sharing must be opt-in. Public cards must not include private balances, portfolio details, account identifiers, email, security state or hidden social data.

Referral value should primarily be cosmetic, collection, prestige or convenience. Do not reward raw signups with meaningful WLD advantage. Eligibility should depend on genuine activation/retention milestones and be reviewed for fake-signup/referral-fraud risk before launch.

## 9. LiveOps and season anticipation

Season communication should create curiosity rather than fear of loss.

- D-14: theme/story reveal and broad aspiration preview.
- D-7: show new collections, fictional companies, jobs/business themes and community projects.
- D-3: personalized “what may matter to you” preview based on explicitly chosen interests where possible.
- D-1: concise reminder with timing and catch-up/late-entry assurance.

After the season:
- preserve archive, titles, selected collections and personal history;
- explain what carries forward;
- show the next season as a new chapter, not a punishment for missing the previous one.

## 10. Content and SEO as retention, not only acquisition

Public content should give people a reason to return even before or between play sessions.

Priority recurring content:
- fictional company explainers and story updates;
- beginner finance/economy learning guides;
- season preview and archive pages;
- collection/lore guides;
- “what changed this week” public world recap;
- educational replay breakdowns.

Each page must stand on its own as useful content. It should not exist only to rank for a keyword or funnel users into registration.

Organic cohorts should be evaluated through:
`organic visit → engaged visit → signup → activation → D7 → D30 → retained revenue/margin`.

## 11. Monetization placement by relationship stage

### Before first value
Avoid disruptive interstitials and aggressive paywalls. Monetization should not obscure whether the product is valuable.

### After repeated value
Eligible offers:
- ad removal;
- non-P2W profile/space/cosmetic expression;
- optional collection presentation tools;
- clearly labeled sponsorship/native content that does not affect WDX/WLD outcomes.

### Established users
Offer identity, archive, space and prestige products that reflect long-term attachment without selling financial-game advantage.

Revenue decisions must track ad-induced churn, D7/D30 changes, subscription cancellation, support burden and cohort margin—not only ad impressions or checkout conversion.

## 12. Trust, abuse and privacy review

### High — public/private boundary leakage
User impact: private portfolio, balances, security or social information could become searchable/shareable.
Abuse: enumeration, stalking, phishing or targeted account attacks.
Minimum condition: only explicitly public-safe fields may appear on public/share/SEO surfaces; account/security/recovery/private asset pages remain non-public and non-indexed.
Separate runtime QA: required before any new public/share surface launches.

### High — comeback/referral phishing and impersonation
User impact: users may trust fake return or reward messages.
Abuse: malicious links imitate Moneyverse comeback/referral messages.
Minimum condition: return messaging must not contain secrets or sensitive asset detail, must use recognizable branding and safe destination rules, and must not pressure users with threats of account/asset loss.
Separate runtime QA: required for actual messaging/deep-link changes.

### Medium — referral/multi-account reward abuse
User impact: economy distortion and degraded social experience.
Minimum condition: no economically meaningful reward for raw signup; use verified activation/retention milestones and non-P2W rewards; monitor fake-signup/referral-fraud signals.

### Medium — social/UGC harassment and doxxing
Minimum condition: privacy controls, block/report paths and moderation readiness before expanding public profile, club or community sharing.

### Medium — analytics/ad overcollection
Minimum condition: do not collect tokens, credentials, private asset data or unnecessary identifiers for retention segmentation; personalized advertising requires separate privacy/age/region review.

Existing authentication, session, RBAC, ledger and security boundaries are not weakened by this growth plan.

## 13. KPI framework

### Core funnel
- visitor → signup conversion;
- signup → first value;
- time-to-first-value;
- first-session completion;
- D1 / D3 / D7 / D14 / D30 retention.

### Return quality
- returning-user share;
- sessions per retained user;
- meaningful actions per session;
- 1–3 minute quick-session completion;
- weekly recap view → meaningful action;
- comeback return rate;
- 7-day self-progress artifact creation/share.

### Long-term attachment
- collection/set progression;
- profession specialization adoption;
- space/profile expression adoption;
- club/community participation;
- season archive engagement;
- voluntary prestige-sink participation.

### Growth and revenue quality
- organic signup → activation → D7/D30;
- share → visit → activation → D7;
- referral → activated retained user;
- CAC and LTV by source;
- ARPU / ARPDAU;
- subscription conversion and cancellation;
- ad-induced churn;
- cohort revenue and contribution margin.

### Trust guardrails
- abuse rate;
- fake-signup rate;
- referral-fraud rate;
- account-takeover signal rate;
- spam/report rate;
- privacy complaint rate;
- suspicious reward-duplication rate.

## 14. Experiment backlog

### Experiment A — D1 continuation card
Hypothesis: a single intent-matched continuation card produces faster meaningful return than a generic dashboard.
Target: first-time activated users returning on D1.
Entry: first authenticated home on return.
Control: normal multi-module home.
Treatment: one primary continuation card + one secondary exploration option.
Primary: D1 meaningful-action completion; time to first meaningful action.
Guardrails: error rate, support complaints, content diversity, abuse signals.
Observation: at least one full weekly cohort; extend if sample is insufficient.
Next: if positive, carry intent continuity into D3; if neutral/negative, test better continuation content rather than more urgency.

### Experiment B — weekly self-progress recap
Hypothesis: self-comparison recap improves D7→D14 return more safely than leaderboard-first recap.
Target: users with 3+ meaningful actions in first week.
Control: standard dashboard/leaderboard summary.
Treatment: personal progress story + optional rank section.
Primary: D14 retention and recap→meaningful-action rate.
Guardrails: user-reported pressure, share privacy complaints, session quality.
Observation: minimum two weekly cycles when feasible.
Next: keep only if retention rises without pressure/privacy deterioration.

### Experiment C — absence-safe comeback copy
Hypothesis: “what changed / what remains / what to do next” outperforms “you missed rewards” without FOMO.
Target: 7–29 day inactive users.
Control: reward-oriented comeback copy where currently used.
Treatment: neutral return briefing + interest-matched next action.
Primary: comeback→meaningful-action conversion and 7-day post-return retention.
Guardrails: unsubscribe/notification opt-out, spam reports, privacy complaints, ATO signals.
Observation: at least two weeks plus post-return retention window.
Next: adopt only if downstream retention improves, not merely open rate.

### Experiment D — shareable weekly story
Hypothesis: a self-progress artifact generates higher-quality referral traffic than generic invite links.
Target: D7+ users who completed meaningful weekly progress.
Control: generic invite/share CTA.
Treatment: optional progress story card with public-safe data only.
Primary: share→engaged visit→activation.
Guardrails: referral fraud, fake signup, report/block rate, privacy complaints.
Observation: at least two weekly cycles and enough activated recipients for directional retention read.
Next: expand only if D7 recipient quality is not worse than baseline acquisition.

### Experiment E — monetization after repeated value
Hypothesis: delaying promotional ad/subscription surfaces until repeated value protects D7 while preserving or improving D30 LTV.
Target: new users.
Control: current eligible monetization timing.
Treatment: suppress disruptive monetization until second meaningful session/clear repeated value, while preserving legally required notices.
Primary: D7 retention and D30 LTV per acquired user.
Guardrails: ARPDAU, subscription conversion, ad-induced churn, support burden.
Observation: long enough to capture D30 economics; early stop for material harm.
Next: optimize placement only after retention impact is understood.
