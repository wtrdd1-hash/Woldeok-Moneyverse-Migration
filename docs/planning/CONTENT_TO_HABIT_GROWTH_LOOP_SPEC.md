## 1. Gap selected

The largest remaining growth gap is not content supply. It is the handoff from useful public content to a durable personal reason to return.

Moneyverse already has plans for pre-signup value, D1–D30 return reasons, shareable progress, and a recurring brand/content cadence. The weak link is that a visitor can read a useful World Brief, company story, beginner guide, season preview, or shared artifact and still leave without forming a personal thread that survives to D7.

This specification therefore optimizes the loop:

`useful content → one understood idea → one low-friction relevant action → one saved personal thread → visible next step → D1 continuation → D3 identity signal → D7 review/return`

The goal is not to increase clicks or force daily visits. It is to convert curiosity into a self-chosen thread the user wants to continue.

## 2. Consumer promise

Every growth surface should answer three questions in sequence:

1. **What did I just understand?**
2. **What can I try or save now?**
3. **Why would I care about seeing what happens next?**

A page that cannot answer these without manipulative urgency should remain a standalone piece of content rather than forcing a product CTA.

## 3. Content-to-action bridge

### 3.1 One page, one primary continuation

Each high-intent public page gets one dominant continuation path, selected from:

- follow a fictional company/sector;
- save a simulated scenario or learning question;
- start a collection chapter;
- preview a profession/business path;
- bookmark a season chapter or world event;
- explore an opt-in club/city/community story;
- continue a shared artifact into a public-safe sample.

Do not show six equally weighted product systems to a first-time reader.

### 3.2 Value before registration

Where safe, allow a 30–90 second preview before account creation:

- choose between two simulated outcomes;
- inspect a fictional-company timeline;
- answer one diversification/risk question;
- assemble one sample collection set;
- preview a season path;
- compare two non-real-money business choices.

The preview must not create authoritative WLD/WDX holdings, private account data, financial promises, or hidden tracking requirements.

### 3.3 Registration as preservation, not ransom

The signup reason should be framed as preserving or continuing something the user already chose:

- `Save this watch idea`
- `Keep this collection path`
- `Continue this learning replay`
- `Remember this season route`
- `Track this fictional company`

Avoid generic `Sign up to continue` when the product can first demonstrate value safely.

## 4. First-session continuation contract

After signup or comeback, do not drop a user on a generic feature dashboard when the entry intent is known.

The first authenticated view should prioritize:

1. the thread that caused entry;
2. one meaningful action that advances it;
3. visible evidence of progress;
4. one next milestone;
5. an optional secondary exploration path.

Examples:

- Beginner diversification article → simulated allocation reflection → saved learning journal → D1 review prompt.
- Fictional-company story → follow company → read next event context → D3 sector preference signal.
- Collection/lore article → start collection chapter → complete one visible slot → D7 collection recap.
- Season preview → choose one season interest → first season activity → D7 progress/catch-up review.

## 5. D1 / D3 / D7 habit bridge

### D1 — continuity
Promise: **“The thing you cared about is still here, and something useful can be continued in a few minutes.”**

Show the saved thread first. Avoid daily-loss language or punishment for not returning exactly on schedule.

### D3 — identity formation
Promise: **“Moneyverse is beginning to reflect what I like.”**

Use behavior to help the user choose a preference, not to trap them in an opaque personalized feed. Examples: collector theme, profession interest, fictional sector interest, learning topic, space/identity preference.

### D7 — reflection and forward pull
Promise: **“I can see what changed, what I learned/built/collected, and what I might want next.”**

Weekly review should emphasize self-comparison and continuity, not wealth ranking or trade frequency. It should contain:
- one personal progress summary;
- one world change relevant to the thread;
- one unfinished aspiration;
- one optional social/share artifact;
- one next action that can be completed quickly.

## 6. Session modes

### 1–3 minute quick check
- inspect one meaningful change;
- make one low-friction choice;
- save/continue the thread;
- leave with the next step visible.

### 5–15 minute meaningful session
- complete one learning, job, collection, business, season, or community milestone;
- create visible progress or a reflection artifact;
- optionally explore one adjacent system.

### 30+ minute deep session
- pursue self-directed collection/space/business/profession planning;
- study replay/journal history;
- contribute to club/city/community goals;
- curate archives, museums, prestige presentation, or long-term identity.

Short-session users must not be structurally punished relative to deep-session users.

## 7. Acquisition and SEO connection

The content portfolio should be evaluated by the quality of the user thread it creates, not by traffic alone.

Priority search journeys:

1. beginner concept query → educational answer → simulated sample → saved learning thread;
2. fictional company/market query → story → follow/explore → D7 company/world revisit;
3. season/event query → preview/archive → choose interest → season continuation;
4. collection/lore query → substantial story → sample chapter → collection aspiration;
5. community/project query → public-safe story → explore relevant club/city experience.

Indexable content must remain useful without signup. Do not generate thin pages solely to manufacture entry points.

Organic quality funnel:

`impression → qualified click → engaged understanding → relevant preview/action → signup/comeback → first meaningful action → D1 → D7 → D30 → contribution margin`

## 8. Social and viral connection

Social proof should reinforce a meaningful thread rather than substitute for it.

Good social artifacts:
- a learning reflection;
- a collection chapter;
- a season progress story;
- a room/office/museum showcase;
- a profession/business milestone;
- a club/city project result;
- a fictional-company thesis or replay result that is clearly simulated.

Do not default to sharing balances, debt, private portfolio positions, casino outcomes, security/account state, or real-world identity.

Referral success is not raw registration. Evaluate `shared context → engaged visit → first value → activation → D7`, with fraud-adjusted attribution.

## 9. Monetization placement

Monetization begins after the page or session has delivered its promised value.

Preferred fits:
- contextual ads after the first useful section, not before the answer;
- ad-free subscription after repeated content/product value is visible;
- non-P2W profile, space, archive, collection, and presentation cosmetics;
- disclosed sponsor support for educational/lore/community series.

Never place ads or sponsor modules so they resemble buy/sell/loan/repay/account-security controls.

Reject a monetization treatment even if short-term revenue rises when it materially worsens activation, D7/D30, trust complaints, accidental clicks, or contribution margin after churn/support costs.

## 11. Experiment backlog

### A. Intent-preserving landing vs generic home
Hypothesis: returning a user to the content-derived thread increases first meaningful action and D7.
Target: new signups from high-intent public content.
Control: generic authenticated home.
Treatment: thread-first continuation view.
Primary: signup → first meaningful action and D7.
Guardrails: confusion/back navigation, support, privacy incidents, errors.
Minimum observation: enough signups for activation comparison plus D7 maturation.
Success: expand by high-intent content pillar. Failure: keep only entry sources with clearly recoverable intent.

### B. Save/continue CTA vs generic signup CTA
Target: anonymous learning/company/collection readers.
Control: `Sign up`.
Treatment: context-specific preservation CTA.
Primary: activated signup rate.
Guardrails: deceptive-copy complaints, fake signup, D7.
Minimum observation: stable signup cohorts plus D7 maturation.
Success: standardize by content archetype. Failure: simplify copy and keep content standalone.

### C. One continuation vs three product choices
Hypothesis: one relevant continuation reduces first-session cognitive load.
Target: first-time content-assisted users.
Primary: time-to-first-value and first-session completion.
Guardrails: exploration breadth by D7, bounce, satisfaction signal.
Minimum observation: first-session sample plus D7 follow-up.
Success: keep one primary + one secondary path. Failure: restore limited choice where intent is genuinely ambiguous.

### D. D7 self-progress review vs leaderboard-first review
Primary: D7 meaningful return and D14 retention.
Guardrails: pressure complaints, risky trading behavior, social-report rate.
Minimum observation: D14 maturation.
Success: use self-progress as default. Failure: test clearer goal framing, not stronger competitive pressure.

### E. Value-first sponsorship vs early sponsorship
Target: public content cohorts.
Control: sponsor before primary answer.
Treatment: sponsor after first useful section/interaction.
Primary: contribution margin per engaged visitor.
Guardrails: engaged read, activation, D7/D30, accidental clicks, trust complaints, ad-induced churn.
Minimum observation: enough revenue volume plus D30 direction where feasible.
Reject early placement if retention/trust harm offsets revenue.

## 12. Security, abuse and privacy review

### High — intent/deep-link abuse and phishing
User impact: a preserved-return experience can be imitated by phishing or abused to redirect users to unsafe destinations.
Minimum protection: only public-safe/internal product destinations in growth journeys; no secret-bearing or sensitive-asset URLs; clear domain/brand identity; no balance/loss threat in comeback copy.
Separate development/QA: required before implementing new authenticated deep-link behavior.

### High — public/private personalization leakage
User impact: content recommendations or shared pages can expose account existence, balances, portfolio interests, social graph, recovery/security state, or inferred sensitive traits.
Minimum protection: explicit public-safe fields, opt-in publication, no private asset/security/moderation data, hide/remove controls.
Separate development/QA: required before personalized public surfaces.

### High — referral/bot reward farming
User impact: fake accounts and automation can extract rewards, distort acquisition metrics, spam users, and damage the economy.
Minimum protection at planning level: no meaningful WLD/WDX reward for page view/share/raw signup; favor cosmetic/prestige recognition after retained/verified value; fraud-adjusted attribution.
Separate development/QA: required before economic referral rewards.

### Medium — analytics/ad overcollection
Do not send credentials, session identifiers, private financial-game fields, recovery/security state, precise sensitive traits, or unnecessary minor data to analytics/ad vendors. Personalized advertising requires separate age/region/privacy review.

### Medium — UGC/community abuse
Public spotlights and share artifacts require reporting, blocking/takedown paths, no forced real identity, and safe outbound-link rules. Doxxing, impersonation, harassment, or malicious links cannot be treated as acceptable growth externalities.

Existing authentication, session, RBAC, ledger, economy, admin, privacy, and Account Security Center boundaries remain unchanged.

## 13. Legal / policy cautions

- WLD/WDX remain virtual/simulated/game-only.
- Educational and fictional-market content must not imply real investment advice, guaranteed returns, deposits, cash redemption, or real securities.
- Sponsored/native/creator relationships require clear Korea/U.S.-appropriate disclosure.
- Personalized advertising, new tracking vendors, known-minor targeting, material economic referral rewards, or expanded public UGC require separate privacy/legal/trust review.
- Public community/person content requires appropriate consent and removal paths.
