## 1. Gap selected

The largest remaining acquisition/retention gap is not another feature. It is that Moneyverse currently has no reliable public recurring content destination that gives a user a reason to revisit when they do not already intend to play.

Production reality on 2026-09-13:
- the public home is reachable and has a `Monthly notes` entry point;
- the public announcements page is reachable;
- both surfaces state that reviewed public notices are still being prepared;
- therefore the previously planned content-return loop is **planned, but not yet observable as a live recurring consumer product**.

This spec narrows the next growth loop to:

`weekly useful world update → understand one change → choose one contextual next action → signup/comeback or continue → meaningful action → D7 return/review`

The Weekly World Brief must be worth reading even when the reader never signs in.

## 2. Consumer promise

**“In a few minutes, understand what changed in Moneyverse this week, why it matters, and what you can explore next.”**

The brief is not a patch-note dump, a promotion feed, a list of rewards the user missed, or a trading-signal newsletter.

Editorial priorities:
1. clarity;
2. novelty with context;
3. continuity of the fictional world;
4. learning and reflection;
5. one optional next action;
6. user control over whether to play, share, subscribe or ignore it.

WLD/WDX, fictional banking, businesses and markets remain virtual/simulated/game-only. Never imply cash redemption, guaranteed returns, real deposits, real securities or investment advice.

## 3. Weekly Brief product shape

A normal edition should take roughly 1–3 minutes to scan and should contain no more material than needed to explain the most meaningful changes.

### 3.1 Required editorial blocks

1. **This week in one sentence**
   - one plain-language summary of the strongest world/content change;
   - no clickbait or fear-of-missing-out language.

2. **Three meaningful changes**
   - select roughly three of: fictional company/sector development, season/event progression, profession/business/world change, collection/lore discovery, club/city/community achievement, beginner learning insight;
   - every item answers `what changed?`, `why is it interesting?`, and `what can I do if I care?`.

3. **One thing worth learning**
   - a short educational concept such as diversification, opportunity cost, supply/demand, sunk-cost awareness, budgeting, market uncertainty, game-economy sink/transfer distinction, or review of a fictional event;
   - never optimize this block for trade count, casino play or borrowing frequency.

4. **One optional next action**
   - contextual rather than generic;
   - examples: read the virtual-company story, preview a collection chapter, continue a profession thread, view the season catch-up, try a safe simulated reflection exercise;
   - avoid presenting five equally loud CTA buttons.

5. **Catch-up line**
   - state that returning late does not erase ordinary progress;
   - link to a concise archive/catch-up path when appropriate;
   - no missed-reward guilt language.

### 3.2 Optional blocks

Use only when there is real substance:
- community or club spotlight based on explicit public-safe participation;
- season preview at D-14/D-7/D-3/D-1;
- collection/lore feature;
- creator/sponsor module with clear disclosure and editorial separation;
- a shareable public-safe summary card.

Do not fill an edition with low-value filler solely to hit a content quota.

## 4. Audience-specific continuation

### Anonymous first-time reader
Goal: understand the service before signup.

Show:
- what Moneyverse is;
- that the economy is fictional/game-only;
- one useful story or learning takeaway;
- one low-friction preview or explanation;
- a contextual signup CTA only after value is delivered.

Do not expose private balances, portfolios, social relationships, account existence, recovery state or security state.

### Signed-in new user
Goal: preserve the thread that caused activation.

The brief should surface one item related to the user's chosen path without requiring the user to understand the entire economy.

### Active returning user
Goal: connect world change to personal continuity.

Prefer `what changed + what you were already doing + one continuation` over an undifferentiated feed of systems.

### Lapsed user
Goal: reduce re-entry cost.

Use:
- `here is what changed`;
- `your ordinary progress remains`;
- `here is one easy place to resume`.

Do not use balance-loss threats, streak punishment or exaggerated urgency.

### Long-term/prestige user
Goal: connect weekly updates to history and legacy.

Highlight archives, collections, spaces, profession/business identity, club/city contribution, museums, season history and public-safe prestige rather than raw wealth ranking.

## 5. Lifecycle role

- **First 30 seconds:** the reader understands one current reason Moneyverse feels alive.
- **First 3 minutes:** the reader finishes one useful story/learning block and sees one natural next step.
- **First session:** content-assisted users should preserve the entry intent through signup and first meaningful action.
- **D1:** show continuity with the first chosen topic or thread.
- **D3:** deepen preference/identity with a related story, collection or learning item.
- **D7:** make the current Weekly World Brief a lightweight return anchor and pair it with a personal-progress review when available.
- **D14:** connect to season/world/community developments without FOMO.
- **D30+:** connect public world history with personal history, archives and long-term aspirations.

## 6. Acquisition and SEO role

The Weekly Brief is a recurring brand/return page, but not every paragraph needs its own indexable URL.

Indexable candidates:
- substantial weekly editions with standalone value;
- durable virtual-company explainers;
- season previews and archives;
- beginner learning guides;
- collection/lore stories;
- substantial opt-in public community/project retrospectives.

Do not index or mass-produce:
- thin auto-generated recap fragments;
- referral/reward pages;
- private portfolio/balance/account/security/recovery/moderation pages;
- personalized recap cards with insufficient standalone value;
- pages created primarily to add ad inventory.

Search success path:

`qualified impression → click → useful understanding → contextual continuation → signup/comeback → activation → D7 → D30 → retention-adjusted contribution`

Search Console's 2026 generative-AI performance reporting should be treated as an additional discovery diagnostic, not as a reason to generate content for AI systems instead of people.

For Naver, prioritize unique titles/descriptions, visitor-first content, clear index/non-index separation and durable RSS/sitemap discoverability for genuinely published editions. Do not use traffic manipulation, thin AI mass generation, cloaking or irrelevant trending keywords.

## 7. Social and viral role

The brief should create shareable **meaning**, not just shareable rewards.

Good share objects:
- one surprising fictional-world change;
- a concise learning takeaway;
- season chapter/lore reveal;
- collection story;
- public-safe club/city achievement;
- an opt-in progress reflection connected to the week's theme.

Bad default share objects:
- private WLD balance;
- portfolio value;
- debt/loan state;
- casino outcome;
- hidden social graph;
- security/account state;
- referral code presented as the main story.

A recipient must be able to understand the shared object before being asked to sign up.

## 8. Monetization policy

The Weekly Brief should first prove repeatable reader value.

Allowed after value is delivered and existing ad policy allows the surface:
- clearly labeled contextual display/native sponsorship;
- sponsor-supported educational/lore module with editorial independence;
- ad-free subscription value;
- non-P2W profile/archive/space/collection presentation products.

Do not monetize an empty or near-empty edition as if it were a content product.

Do not place ads adjacent to buy/sell/borrow/repay/casino confirmation-like controls. Sponsors cannot buy WDX outcomes, market conclusions, ranking advantage, loan terms, moderation priority or disguised organic recommendations.

Success requires retention-adjusted contribution, not maximum impressions.

## 9. Funnel and KPI model

Primary content-return funnel:

`public home/search/share → Weekly Brief view → engaged read → contextual next action → signup/comeback/continue → meaningful action → D1/D7 → D30`

Core KPIs:
- Weekly Brief unique readers;
- engaged-reader rate;
- 7-day and 30-day brief revisit rate;
- brief → contextual-action rate;
- brief → signup/comeback → activation;
- brief-assisted D1/D7/D14/D30;
- content-assisted time-to-first-value;
- returning-user share;
- branded/direct return share;
- organic signup → activation → D7;
- share → engaged visit → activation → D7;
- content-pillar D30/LTV;
- ARPU/ARPDAU, sponsor/ad yield, subscription conversion and retention-adjusted contribution.

Trust/security guardrails:
- fake-signup rate;
- bot/engagement fraud rate;
- referral fraud rate;
- account-takeover signal rate;
- spam/report/takedown rate;
- privacy complaint rate;
- accidental-ad-click/trust complaint rate;
- suspicious reward duplication rate;
- misleading-finance-claim complaint rate.

## 10. Cohorts

Always compare at minimum:
- first-time vs returning anonymous readers;
- organic vs direct vs social/share vs referral vs paid source;
- signed-in new users vs established users vs lapsed users;
- mobile vs desktop;
- Korean vs English/other locale when applicable;
- content pillar;
- users exposed vs not exposed to monetization;
- privacy/consent state when relevant and lawful.

Do not declare a content channel successful on traffic volume if its activation, D7/D30, trust or contribution margin is weak.

## 11. Experiment backlog

### Experiment A — Weekly Brief vs empty/notice-only return surface
Hypothesis: a concise useful weekly edition creates qualified returns and more meaningful continuation than a generic operations notice page.
Target: public home/announcement visitors.
Control: current notice-only/empty experience once an equivalent control can be observed.
Treatment: one editorial Weekly Brief with a single contextual continuation.
Primary metric: engaged read → meaningful action/comeback.
Guardrails: bounce, D7, privacy complaints, misleading-finance complaints, ad-induced churn.
Minimum observation: at least 3–4 weekly publication cycles plus D7 maturation; interpret small samples directionally.
Success: continue the cadence and deepen the strongest pillar. Failure: reduce scope and preserve only the highest-value block.

### Experiment B — one contextual CTA vs generic multi-link CTA set
Hypothesis: one context-matched next action lowers cognitive load.
Target: first-time and returning brief readers.
Primary: meaningful-action rate and time-to-first-value.
Guardrails: next-page bounce, D1/D7, error rate.
Minimum observation: enough traffic for stable directional comparison plus D7 maturation.
Success: adopt per content pillar. Failure: add a secondary exploration link without returning to feature-grid overload.

### Experiment C — world-change + explanation vs raw change list
Hypothesis: explanatory context produces deeper engagement and return intent than patch-note style lists.
Primary: completion, save/share, contextual action, 7-day revisit.
Guardrails: reading time inflation without action, support confusion, finance-like claim complaints.
Minimum observation: multiple editions covering different themes.

### Experiment D — public-safe share card vs generic referral invitation
Hypothesis: meaningful content artifacts produce higher-quality recipient activation than reward-first referral messaging.
Primary: share → engaged visit → activation → D7.
Guardrails: spam/report, referral fraud, privacy complaints.
Minimum observation: D7-matured recipient cohorts.

### Experiment E — monetization after useful block vs earlier monetization
Hypothesis: delayed monetization preserves content completion and downstream retention while maintaining acceptable yield.
Primary: retention-adjusted contribution.
Guardrails: bounce, accidental click, D7/D30, CWV, trust complaints.
Minimum observation: enough sessions for both revenue and downstream retention comparison.

## 12. Security, abuse and privacy review

### High — public/private boundary leakage
User impact: stalking, targeted phishing, embarrassment, account targeting.
Abuse scenario: a public weekly story or personalized share card accidentally exposes balance, portfolio, social relationship, moderation/security or account-recovery context.
Minimum protection: public-safe fields only, explicit opt-in for personalized public content, redact/hide/delete path, no secret/session/recovery data in URLs or cards.
Separate development/QA: required before personalized public or share surfaces.

### High — official-news impersonation and phishing
User impact: credential theft or account takeover.
Abuse scenario: attackers imitate a Weekly Brief, season notice or reward message and send a fake login/reward link.
Minimum protection: consistent official-domain/brand cues, no secret-bearing links, no sensitive balance/loss details in notification copy, no urgency based on account loss.
Separate development/QA: required for email/push/deep-link campaigns.

### High — finance-like claim drift
User impact: users may misunderstand a fictional economy as real financial guidance or guaranteed returns.
Abuse scenario: a fictional-company story becomes `buy now`, `safe yield`, `guaranteed passive income` or loss-recovery messaging.
Minimum protection: game-only disclosure near relevant content, editorial review, no real-return guarantees, no investment-advice positioning.
Separate legal/product review: required if asset characteristics ever move toward real financial products or cash redemption.

### Medium — engagement/referral farming
Do not give meaningful WLD/WDX for page views, shares or raw registrations. Use downstream retained milestones and fraud-adjusted attribution if rewards are later considered.

### Medium — UGC/community spotlight abuse
Require public consent, report/block/takedown, anti-doxxing rules, safe outbound-link handling and no forced real-world identity.

### Medium — analytics/ad overcollection
Do not send tokens, credentials, private economy state, security context or unnecessary identifiers to third-party analytics/ad systems. Personalization or minor targeting requires separate age/region/privacy review.

Existing auth/session/RBAC/ledger/admin/privacy boundaries remain unchanged.

## 13. Legal and policy cautions

- WLD/WDX remain virtual/simulated/game-only.
- No cash redemption, real-security, deposit or guaranteed-return implication.
- Sponsored/native content and compensated creator relationships require clear Korea/U.S.-appropriate disclosure.
- Personalized advertising, new tracking vendors, minor targeting, economically meaningful referral rewards or large-scale UGC require separate privacy/legal/trust review.
- Subscription offers must keep material terms clear, consent explicit and cancellation straightforward under applicable current law/policy.
- Community spotlight publication needs appropriate consent and takedown handling.

## 14. External research note

Research date: 2026-09-13.

### Directly adopted

1. **Spotify Newsroom, 2026-07-10, official product update — weekly discovery playlists.**
   - Finding: Release Radar reaches nearly 9 million listeners weekly; Spotify combines a predictable weekly cadence with personalization and user controls.
   - Adoption: use a predictable weekly discovery anchor and let readers follow the context they care about. Do not use Spotify engagement numbers as Moneyverse forecasts.

2. **Discord, 2026-08-20, official product/press update — game discovery and social play.**
   - Finding: Discord is connecting game discovery, social context and downstream play/retention rather than treating discovery as a one-off impression.
   - Adoption: judge public content by meaningful continuation and retention, not views alone. Discord's reported causal metrics are reference evidence only, not Moneyverse projections.

3. **Google Search Central, 2026-06-03 with worldwide rollout noted 2026-08-31, official Search Console update.**
   - Finding: Search Console now exposes dedicated visibility reporting for generative-AI features in Search and Discover.
   - Adoption: add AI-search visibility as a diagnostic alongside ordinary organic performance while continuing to optimize for useful human content and downstream activation/retention.

4. **Naver Search Advisor, current 2026 guidance, official search guidance.**
   - Finding: pages should be made primarily for visitors, use clear unique titles/descriptions, explicitly separate indexable and non-indexable content, and avoid thin mass-generated/manipulated traffic content.
   - Adoption: publish only substantial weekly/archive pages and preserve private-page exclusion.

### Reference / guardrail

5. **Google Search Central, 2026-08-28 Site Reputation Policy update.**
   - Use as a guardrail against turning sponsor/creator content into reputation-renting SEO inventory.

6. **Current FTC native-advertising and endorsement principles.**
   - Use for clear sponsor/material-connection disclosure; no hidden paid financial-game recommendations.
