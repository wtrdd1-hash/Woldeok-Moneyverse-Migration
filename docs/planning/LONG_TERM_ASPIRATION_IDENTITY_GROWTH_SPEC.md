## 1. Gap selected

The largest remaining consumer-growth gap is the answer to: **why does a user still care after D30?**

Moneyverse already has plans for pre-signup value, D1–D30 return reasons, content-to-habit continuation, seasons, sharing, collections, spaces, businesses, clubs, museums, archives and prestige sinks. But those elements are still spread across multiple systems. A long-term player can understand what to do today without clearly seeing what kind of person, collection, place, institution or historical record they are building over months.

This specification turns those systems into a consumer-facing aspiration ladder:

`first meaningful memory → chosen identity → visible body of work → curated collection/space → community contribution → seasonal history → legacy/prestige goal → new chapter`

The objective is not endless numerical escalation. It is durable attachment through identity, memory, authorship and unfinished aspiration.

## 2. Long-term consumer promise

A returning user should eventually be able to say:

- **I have a history here.** My earlier seasons, decisions and milestones are still legible.
- **This looks like mine.** My profile, collection, room/office/museum and chosen themes express taste rather than raw wealth alone.
- **I am becoming something.** Profession, collector, builder, analyst, entrepreneur, curator or community identity gains coherence over time.
- **I helped shape something larger.** Club/city/community projects can leave a visible non-cash legacy.
- **There is still a next chapter.** Completion creates curation, prestige or a new branch rather than a dead end.

Do not make long-term retention depend primarily on fear of losing streaks, decaying assets, expiring wealth, punitive taxes or inaccessible limited-time rewards.

## 3. Aspiration ladder by maturity

### 3.1 New user — “I started something”
Primary aspirations:
- first collection chapter;
- first profession identity;
- first fictional-company/watch idea;
- first personal space theme;
- first season memory.

Desired emotion: **ownership of a beginning**.

The product should show one unfinished next milestone rather than the full endgame catalog.

### 3.2 Developing user — “I have a recognizable style”
Primary aspirations:
- specialization inside a profession/business path;
- completing coherent collection sets rather than accumulating random inventory;
- improving a room, office or display space;
- building a personal learning/market journal;
- contributing to a club/city project;
- earning a season chapter that becomes permanent history.

Desired emotion: **identity and taste**.

### 3.3 Established user — “I have a body of work”
Primary aspirations:
- museum/archive curation;
- restoration, engraving and provenance storytelling;
- headquarters or signature space;
- multi-season record;
- long-running business or profession legacy;
- meaningful community/project contribution history;
- rare but non-P2W presentation layers.

Desired emotion: **authorship and accumulated meaning**.

### 3.4 Prestige user — “I can leave a legacy without needing to dominate others”
Primary aspirations:
- Hall of Fame / archive recognition based on transparent, abuse-resistant criteria;
- landmark, city, museum or community patronage that is clearly virtual and does not buy governance or economic advantage;
- high-end restoration/curation/presentation projects;
- collection masterwork goals;
- season anthology and historical exhibits;
- mentorship/educational recognition where safe;
- optional reset/new-chapter paths that preserve history and cosmetics instead of erasing identity.

Desired emotion: **legacy, stewardship and renewed purpose**.

Prestige must not require the user to become richer than everyone else, trade more frequently, gamble more, take larger virtual loans, or pay real money for competitive superiority.

## 4. Personal history as a retention asset

Moneyverse should preserve and surface meaningful history at multiple cadences:

- **weekly:** what changed, what I continued, one next aspiration;
- **seasonal:** chapter summary, collections, profession/business milestones, community contributions, learning moments;
- **annual/long-period:** a personal Moneyverse retrospective that shows themes, creations, collections, spaces, seasons and chosen identities;
- **lifetime:** archive/museum/profile history that the user can curate rather than an endless raw event log.

The recap should emphasize interpretation and story, not just activity volume.

Avoid glorifying:
- raw trade count;
- casino frequency or net win/loss;
- debt size;
- total WLD wealth as the default prestige measure;
- excessive session time as inherently better.

Good recap dimensions include:
- collections completed or curated;
- professions explored/mastered;
- spaces built or redesigned;
- fictional sectors studied;
- learning reflections completed;
- seasons participated in;
- club/city projects supported;
- artifacts restored/engraved;
- personally selected “favorite moment.”

## 5. Collection and curation loop

Long-term collection value should evolve through five stages:

1. **Acquire** — obtain an item, chapter or memory.
2. **Understand** — lore, provenance, season context or learning meaning becomes visible.
3. **Complete** — sets/themes create a satisfying milestone.
4. **Curate** — user chooses what to display, group, restore, engrave or archive.
5. **Reinterpret** — old material becomes relevant in a new season, exhibition, room or personal retrospective without invalidating original history.

This creates reasons to spend WLD voluntarily on expression, restoration, space and prestige while preserving unlimited-by-default play. The economy goal is “I want to improve how my history looks and what it represents,” not “the system must confiscate my excess balance.”

## 6. Seasonal continuity without FOMO

Every season should contribute to permanent identity through at least one of:
- archive chapter;
- collection/lore record;
- title/badge/presentation layer;
- personal retrospective entry;
- club/city contribution history;
- optional cosmetic or space artifact.

Users joining late or returning after absence should be able to understand prior seasons without being told their account is permanently ruined.

Season preview cadence (D-14 / D-7 / D-3 / D-1) should answer:
- What is new?
- Which existing identities/collections become interesting again?
- What can a late or returning user still enjoy?
- What will remain in the permanent archive afterward?

Do not use shrinking timers or “lose everything” language as the main retention mechanism.

## 7. Social and viral value

Long-term identity creates more defensible sharing than raw referral links.

Preferred share artifacts:
- seasonal chapter cards;
- before/after space or museum transformations;
- collection completion stories;
- profession/business milestones;
- learning-year summaries;
- club/city project completion stories;
- multi-season anthology covers;
- user-selected favorite memories.

A recipient should understand the artifact without an account and see one relevant public-safe continuation, not an immediate signup wall.

Do not expose private balances, debt, private portfolio positions, security/recovery state, hidden social graph, moderation status or real-world identity by default.

## 8. SEO and durable public content

Long-term systems can create durable search value only when pages contain substantial public context.

Index candidates:
- season archives with original editorial context;
- fictional-company histories;
- curated collection/lore guides;
- educational retrospectives explaining simulated concepts;
- public club/city project stories with explicit publication permission;
- substantial public museums/showcases where the owner opted in.

Do not mass-index thin automatically generated user recap cards, private profiles, asset histories, portfolio pages or reward/referral pages.

Organic quality funnel:

`search/discovery → useful history/lore/guide → meaningful exploration → signup/comeback → identity milestone → D30 → multi-season return → contribution margin`

For finance-adjacent content, authorship, source quality, virtual/simulated disclaimers and factual review matter more than keyword volume.

## 9. Monetization after attachment

Long-term attachment creates natural non-P2W monetization surfaces:
- expanded presentation themes;
- profile/archive/museum visual themes;
- room/office/headquarters cosmetic packs;
- additional curation/presentation styles that do not grant economic advantage;
- ad-free subscription after repeated value is established;
- clearly disclosed sponsorship of lore, education or cultural/community series.

Never sell:
- better WDX outcomes;
- higher loan eligibility or lower loan cost;
- ranking advantage;
- moderation priority;
- integrity-review bypass;
- economic superiority disguised as prestige.

Subscription value should be understandable before purchase and cancellation must not depend on retention dark patterns. Treat subscription friction as a trust risk, not a growth tactic.

## 10. KPI additions

### Long-term retention
- D30 / D60 / D90 / multi-season retention where sample size permits;
- returning-user share;
- seasons-per-retained-user;
- archive/retrospective revisit rate;
- percentage with at least one self-chosen long-term aspiration;
- aspiration continuation rate after 30/60/90 days;
- collection curation rate, not only acquisition count;
- personal-space revisit/redesign rate;
- club/city contribution repeat rate;
- comeback → identity-thread continuation rate.

### Viral/brand
- retrospective/share artifact → engaged visit → activation → D7;
- share rate among retained users;
- branded/direct return share;
- organic visits to evergreen archives/lore;
- public artifact hide/remove/report rate.

### Profitability
- D30/D90 LTV by aspiration cohort;
- subscription conversion after demonstrated repeat value;
- cosmetic/presentation revenue per retained user;
- ad-induced churn;
- contribution margin after content, infrastructure, support and payment costs.

### Trust/abuse guardrails
- fake prestige/referral farming rate;
- suspicious reward duplication;
- multi-account/collusion signals around community recognition;
- privacy complaint rate;
- public/private exposure incidents;
- impersonation/doxxing/malicious-link reports;
- ATO signal rate;
- sponsor-disclosure complaints.

## 11. Experiment backlog

### A. Long-term aspiration selection vs generic next action
Hypothesis: letting a D7–D14 user choose one visible long-term goal increases D30 without increasing pressure.
Target: activated users with at least one meaningful thread.
Control: generic recommended actions.
Treatment: choose one aspiration such as collection, profession, space, season history, learning or community project.
Primary: D30 retention and aspiration continuation.
Guardrails: choice regret, feature abandonment, support/confusion, pressure complaints.
Observation: D30-matured cohort; directional D60 when available.
Success: expand aspiration framing. Failure: make aspirations more reversible and concrete.

### B. Curated retrospective vs activity-stat recap
Hypothesis: story/identity recap creates stronger return and sharing than raw activity volume.
Target: users with at least 30 days of history.
Control: numerical activity summary.
Treatment: curated story emphasizing collections, spaces, learning, seasons and user-selected favorite moment.
Primary: recap revisit + meaningful return within 7 days.
Guardrails: privacy complaints, sharing regret, risky-behavior amplification.
Observation: at least one full recap cycle.

### C. Collection completion → curation goal vs next acquisition prompt
Primary: D60/D90 collection retention and voluntary sink participation.
Guardrails: spend pressure, wealth inequality in presentation, complaint rate.
Success: use curation as the default post-completion chapter.

### D. Permanent season archive vs stronger expiring-reward messaging
Primary: late/returning-user season participation and post-season return.
Guardrails: FOMO/pressure complaints, bot/fraud behavior, reward inflation.
Success: keep permanent-history framing as default.

### E. Value-established subscription prompt vs early subscription prompt
Target: users with repeated meaningful sessions.
Primary: subscription conversion and contribution margin.
Guardrails: D30/D90, cancellation complaints, support contacts, ad-induced/subscription-induced churn.
Observation: enough conversion volume plus retained cohort maturation.

## 12. Security, abuse and privacy review

### High — public archive/profile leakage
User impact: a showcase or retrospective can unintentionally reveal balances, portfolio interests, social graph, account age, behavior patterns or security/recovery information.
Minimum protection: public-safe fields only; explicit opt-in for public artifacts; private by default for sensitive history; hide/remove controls; no security/recovery/moderation data.
Separate development/QA: required before new personalized public surfaces.

### High — prestige/community manipulation
User impact: bots, collusion or multi-accounting can manufacture recognition, referral value or community legitimacy.
Minimum planning condition: prestige should emphasize diversified, verifiable, non-cash contribution and curation rather than one farmable counter; meaningful economic rewards should not attach to raw views/shares/rank.
Separate development/QA: required before material rewards or governance effects.

### High — phishing/impersonation through recap/share pages
Minimum protection: clear first-party branding/domain, no secret-bearing URLs, no urgent asset-loss language, no credential prompts embedded in shared artifacts, safe outbound-link policy.
Separate development/QA: required for new deep-link/share authentication flows.

### Medium — behavioral profiling/advertising overreach
Long-term history can reveal strong inferred preferences. Do not turn archives, learning patterns, minors’ behavior or finance-adjacent activity into unrestricted ad-targeting data. New personalized advertising or tracking vendors require separate privacy/legal review.

Existing auth, session, RBAC, ledger, economy, privacy and Account Security Center boundaries remain unchanged.

## 13. Legal and policy cautions

- WLD/WDX remain virtual/simulated/game-only.
- Retrospectives must not imply real investment performance, guaranteed returns, deposits, securities or cash redemption.
- Sponsored/creator/community recognition must disclose material relationships where applicable.
- Subscription offers require clear material terms, informed consent and straightforward cancellation; U.S. negative-option requirements are evolving and require launch-time legal review.
- Public community/user artifacts require consent, removal paths and conservative handling of minors.
- Material economic referral/prestige rewards, expanded public UGC, personalized advertising or new tracking vendors require separate privacy/legal/trust review.

## 14. External research adopted

Research date: 2026-09-13.

Directly adopted / directional evidence:
1. Spotify, `2025 Wrapped Is Here With More Layers, Stories, and Connection Than Ever Before` (2025-12-03): personalized history is presented as a story users can revisit and share; supports identity-first retrospective design.
2. Spotify Q4 2025 earnings update (2026-02-10): Spotify reported more than 300M engaged Wrapped users and more than 630M global social shares. Treated as evidence that personally meaningful retrospectives can become major brand/share surfaces, not as a Moneyverse forecast.
3. PlayStation, `2025 Wrap-Up` (2025-12-09): personalized achievements, genres, play history, trophy milestones and a completion avatar support combining reflection with a small commemorative artifact.
4. Spotify 2025 Wrapped Clubs (2025-12-03): transforms behavioral history into a playful identity/role. Moneyverse adopts the identity principle but not opaque high-stakes profiling.
5. Google Search Central people-first content guidance: useful, original, trustworthy content should satisfy visitors independently of ranking manipulation; especially relevant to finance-adjacent/YMYL-like topics.
6. Naver Search Advisor current content/spam guidance: prioritize real user value; avoid thin mass-generated, misleading, phishing-like or search-only content.
7. FTC Shutterstock subscription settlement (2026-05): clear material terms, informed consent and simple cancellation are relevant trust constraints for any ad-free/subscription offer.

Reference-only:
- Wrapped Party and similar social comparison patterns show identity can become social, but Moneyverse should not require live competition or public comparison for long-term retention.
